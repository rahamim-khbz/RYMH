הענף הב': ביאור אגרת רב שרירא גאון — Branch Two: Elucidation of Rav Sherira Gaon's Epistle
שערי תשובה סי' קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

הלזה, והוא, שתכף אחר חתימת המשנה, ופטי' רבי, כבר החלו ללקוט ולסדר התלמוד שבידם, בלשון אחד, ופה אחד, ואנו יודעים שסידור התלמוד הוא מהאמוראים, ולא מהתנאים, ובדור שלאחרי פטירת רבי עדיין היו תנאים, וא"כ איך יתקיימו שני הכללים האמתיים הללו? לזה הוכרח להפסיק הדיבור ולהודיע לנו, שאפי' שהיו נקראים תנאים שהיה להם כח לפלוגי, כדאמרינן רב תנא ופליג, עכ"פ לא היה כחם יפה להוסיף מתניתין, וברייתא, כי כל דבריהם תלמוד יתקרי, ולא משנה וברייתא, ואגב אורחיה בזה יודיע לנו זמן סוף המשנה, ומי הם הנקראים בשם כלול של תנאים ואמוראים ביחד, ומי הם שלא נקראו כי אם אמוראים לבר, ומכלל דבריו הודיע לנו הגאון שלשלת יחוסו שהוא מרבה בר אבוה, ואח"ך הוא הגאון הולך ויורד עם הדורות הבאים בזה אחר זה עד שהגיע לזמן נחותי בבל, והודיע לנו מי הם היו דסלקין ונחתין, ואיך בזמן אביי ורבא נפיש שמדא בא"י ואמעיטא הוראה תמן שר"ל שמרוב הצרות, הנשארים שם נטפלו בעניני הדרשות והרמזים, ולא בעוקר הדינים והחכמה התלמודיית המאירת עינים, כאשר נראה מכל המדרשים שכולם כמעט הם אגדות א"י וז"א הגאון ז"ל: "ובתר רבי הוו תנאי מן התם (מא"י) כגון ר' יונתן (כצ"ל עיין סדר הדורות ריש אות יו"ד) ור"ש ור"ג ברבי, ומן הכא (כ) ר' יאשיה דמן הוצל מיהו לא תני מידי ממתניתין (ר"ל לא חדשו דבר במשנה וברייתא) אלא ההוא דהוה תניא קמי הכי, כדמפורש בספרו של אדם הראשון (כא) ר' ור' נתן סוף משנה, והוו נמי רבנן אחריני דאיתינון תנאי ואמוראי כגון וכו', ואית

English Translation

to this, namely, that immediately after the redaction of the Mishnah and the passing of Rabbi [Judah the Prince], they already began to collect and arrange the Talmud at their disposal, with one language and one voice. And we know that the arrangement of the Talmud is from the Amoraim, and not from the Tannaim. Yet in the generation following the passing of Rabbi, there were still Tannaim. If so, how can these two true principles be reconciled? To this, he was compelled to interrupt the discourse and inform us that even though they were called Tannaim, possessing the power to dispute, as we say, 'Rav was a Tanna and disputed,' nevertheless, their power was not sufficient to add Mishnah or Baraita, for all their words would be called Talmud, and not Mishnah or Baraita. And incidentally, by this, he informs us of the time of the conclusion of the Mishnah, and who are those called by the inclusive title of Tannaim and Amoraim together, and who were called only Amoraim separately. And from his words, the Gaon informed us of his chain of lineage, that he is from Rabbah bar Avuha. And afterward, the Gaon traces the generations, descending one after another, until he reached the time of the *Neḥutei Bavel* (those who went down to Babylonia). And he informed us who were those who went up and went down, and how in the time of his father and Rabbah, apostasy greatly increased in the Land of Israel, and halakhic instruction there diminished, meaning that due to the abundance of troubles, those who remained there occupied themselves with matters of allegories and allusions, and not with the fundamental laws and the eye-enlightening Talmudic wisdom, as is evident from all the Midrashim, which are almost all the letters of Yonatan. And the Gaon, of blessed memory, said: "And after Rabbi [Judah the Prince], there were Tannaim from there, such as Rabbi Shimon and Rabban Gamliel bar Rabbi, and from here, Rabbi Yoshiya of Hutzal." However, they did not teach anything from the Mishnah or Baraita, but only that which had already been taught before, as is explicitly stated in the Book of Adam the First: "Rabbi [Judah the Prince] and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah." And there were also other Rabbis who were both Tannaim and Amoraim, such as, etc.
And there are Rabbis. This is another proof that there was writing, for this collection and arrangement is impossible without writing, except that the prohibition applies to learning from the written text. And concerning this, he aptly concluded, "to study it orally." And this is evidence for my previous distress concerning the emendation "and the Rabbis of *Hakhah*" (meaning Babylonia, the place of our Gaon, of blessed memory). The error between "of *Hakhah*" and "of *Babba*" is quite prone to occur, as is understood. And this is a conjecture close to the truth, that the copy of *Ḥofesh Matmonim* is later than that of *Yuḥasin*. And see there "of *Kabba*" and in its place "of *Bavel*." My intention here is to adduce further proof that the *Yuḥasin* copy proceeds in its entirety, and if errors occurred in it, a youth would write them. And the *Ḥofesh Matmonim* copy is the one that follows it, and it is the one that trampled and corrupted several things, whether intentionally, or in error, or due to limited understanding, or due to the copyist's complete lack of knowledge regarding the precision of writing and language, and ultimately due to a lack of wisdom and linguistic order, until the entire copy was covered with thistles upon thistles. And may God atone for him, for he surely erred in seeing and judged wrongly.
And again, after this, I examined *Toldot Rabban Nissim ben Adiel* by the Gaon, may his light shine, page 32, which preceded the printing of *Ḥofesh Matmonim* by fifteen years. He also, may his light shine, emended "and the Rabbis of Bavel," and it is wondrous for its perfect beauty. How can one who counts the footsteps of Rabbi Menachem Botarel, of blessed memory, in precise scrutinies, one of them from the word "Bavel," emend here "of *bekhol*" and not "of *Hakhah*"? Be that as it may, God has graciously brought to my hand another pleasing reinforcement for my conjecture that the text of *Ḥofesh Matmonim* is derived from the text of *Yuḥasin*, with changes that were made deliberately. And it is he who transferred "of *Babba*" to "of *Bavel*", as the Gaon, may his light shine, emended, etc. More recently here, when I was in Vienna, I found in its library a small collection of responsa of the Geonim, and it is collection 111, where it is written as follows:
"A question to the Gaon: And you asked whether the book of Adam the First is extant or not. It was not seen by us nor praised by us, nor is it a false matter, but rather, there is in it an explanation of generations and their sages, as with Shemuel Yarḥinaah, and it explains that Rabbi [Judah the Prince] and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah, and Ravina and Rav Ashi are the end of instruction. And in the Aggadah of Vayikra in the Midrash Tanhuma, in Parashat Tazria, that when it is revealed, the Messiah King will not come until all the souls that were conceived in thought to be created are created, and these are the souls mentioned in the book of Adam the First; this is the Book of Generations of Adam." Thus concludes the text of the responsum I found written. And I searched in Midrash Tanhuma in Parashat Tazria, and it is not there, but it is in Bereshit Rabbah, chapter 24. And the main statement from which he should have derived support was that which is brought there in Bereshit Rabbah above this, on the verse, "Your eyes saw my unformed body" (Psalms 139:16) – the unformed body that your eyes saw, they are already written in the book of Adam the First; this is the Book of Generations. And above this, it says that the Holy One, blessed be He, showed Adam the First every generation and its expounders, and so forth. And what may be said is that our master, the Gaon, already brought something superior to this, which is that of Shemuel Yarḥinaah in Perek HaPoʿalim, "For my part, I have seen the book of Adam the First," and it is written therein: "Shemuel Yarḥinaah will be called a Sage, but he will not be called a Rabbi." And there is no doubt that this manuscript question is also from Kairouan, where they asked our Gaon, of blessed memory, concerning that before us, which he sent to them, wherein it is written, "as explicitly stated in the book of Adam the First: Rabbi [Judah the Prince] and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah." Concerning this, they asked him if this book is extant. For indeed, regarding what they said in the Talmud, "For my part, I have seen the book of Adam the First," it is good that we can say it was extant among them. But that the Gaon should say, "as explicitly stated," etc., as if it were under his hand, and that it explicitly states concerning Rabbi [Judah the Prince] and Rabbi Natan – this is astonishing! And it is proper to understand this. And to this, he replied to them with profound wisdom that no such book existed or was created, but rather, every matter in which we see that we have striven with all our might and it has not come to light, then we are compelled to say that it thus arose in primordial thought. As with Shemuel Yarḥinaah, who strove with all his might, but ordination was not granted to him. And he said, "For my part, I have seen it," meaning, I saw in my destiny that despite all the toil and effort, ordination was not granted to me; behold, it is as if it were written in a book. And so here, after Rabbi [Judah the Prince] strove to arrange the Mishnahs and to ease study, it was natural that the matter would continue for three or four generations after him, and the thread of grace from the generations of the Tannaim would not be severed from it. Yet we saw that despite all his efforts to magnify Torah and glorify it, nevertheless, in him, the thread was severed, and he was the end of the Mishnah. Thus, it is found that it is as explicitly stated in the book of Adam the First: Rabbi [Judah the Prince] and Rabbi [Natan]...
