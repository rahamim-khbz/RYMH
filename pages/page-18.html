תשובת הגאון: סידור המשנה וטעמיה — The Gaon's Response: The Arrangement of the Mishnah and its Rationale
שה

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

ועכשיו שכבר ביאר לנו הגאון, הב' רוחים שהרוחנו במלאכת רבינו הקדוש, והוא: פישוט הספקות, ובירורן וכתיבתן בחיבור, מה שלא היה לראשונים, כי אם זכרון שמועות.
עוד הולך ומוסיף להודיענו, שעוד ריוח שלישי הרוחנו במלאכתו הקדושה והוא אומרו: וכן נמי לא הוו גרסי (אלו הראשונים שהזכיר למעלה) כולא (ר"ל מתניתין) בפה אחד ולשון אחד, אלא טעמייהו הוו ידעין להון וכלהון דעת אחת הוה בהון" ר"ל שאפי' שכל אחד שונה בלשון משונה מחברו עכ"פ הדעת דעת אחד לאסור או להתיר וכיוצא, וזה הריוח שהרוחנו במלאכת רבינו הקדוש שהכל לשון אחד ופה אחד, ועכשיו מוסיף ומודיע הריוח הד' והוא: "ולא הות בגירסיהו פלוגתא (יוחסין, ובח"מ חסר ונמצאו הדברים מבולבלים ובלתי מובנים ודוק) וידעי מאי דאיתיה דברי הכל, ומאי דאיתיה פלוגתא, ומאי דאיתיה ליחיד, ומאי דאיתיה לרבים" ר"ל שאפי' שהיו יודעים, איזה דין הוא דברי הכל, ואיזה דין יש בו פלוגתא, ויודעים ג"כ צדדי המחלוקת, לאיזה צד הוא סברת היחיד, ולאיזה צד סברת הרבים, עכ"ז לא היו גורסין בגירסתם הדין עם המחלוקת שלו, כמו שהוא היום בסדר משנתנו שתקן רבי, וזהו הריוח הד'. ומוסיף עוד הריוח החמישי והוא: "ולא היו להם דברים מתוקנים, ומשנה ידועה וכו' עד כל אחד ואחד מתני לתלמודיו באיזה דיבור (ד) שירצה, ובאיזה דרך שירצה יש אוחז דרך קצרה וכו' ואית (ח"מ חסר) דתני כללות, ואית דפרטין, ואית דמרווחין (ח"מ, וביוחסין "מומחין" טעות) ופשטין דמויי ודמויי דדמויי" ר"ל דמלבד דהרוחנו פשיטות הספקות

English Translation

Now that the Gaon has already elucidated for us the two benefits that we have derived from the work of Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh, namely, the resolution of doubts, their clarification, and their inscription in a compilation – a condition not enjoyed by the Rishonim, who possessed only the recollection of traditions. He proceeds further to apprise us of yet a third benefit gained through his sacred labor, which is his assertion: "And similarly, they did not yet study all of it with one mouth and one tongue; rather, they knew their reasons, and all of them were of one mind concerning them." Meaning to say, that even if each one taught in a wording different from his colleague, nevertheless the understanding was one, whether to prohibit or to permit, and so forth. This is the benefit that we have derived from the work of Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh, that everything is in one wording and with one mouth. And now he further adds and informs us of the fourth benefit, which is: "And there was no controversy in their study. And they knew what constituted a unanimous opinion, and what constituted a dispute, and what belonged to an individual, and what belonged to the many." Meaning to say, that even though they knew which ruling was a unanimous opinion, and which ruling involved a dispute, and they also understood the various sides of the controversy, to which side the individual's opinion inclined, and to which side the opinion of the many inclined, nevertheless, they did not study the ruling along with its accompanying controversy in their learning, as is the practice today in the systematic arrangement of our Mishnah, which Rabbi [Yehudah ha-Nasi] established. And this is the fourth benefit. And he further adds the fifth benefit, which is: "And they possessed no fixed formulations, nor a known Mishnah, and so forth, until each one taught his students in whatever wording he desired, and in whatever manner he desired; some adopted a concise method, and so forth. And there are those who taught general principles, and there are those who taught details, and there are those who expounded, and they would simplify analogies and analogies of analogies."
Meaning to say, that besides our having gained the resolution of doubts and their clarification — for the Gaon is not a peddler to reiterate that the Rishonim did not possess these doubts, as he has already stated this countless times. But the author of this particular version had it within his power to omit this entire line, as you will observe below, for he omitted many things without hesitation. Rather, he perceived that no connection would remain for the words he subsequently states: "And similarly, they also did not study with one mouth," which refers back to the Rishonim. And if he were to omit this entire subject, these words would then refer to those mentioned immediately prior, and it would appear that they are the generation of Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh, who indeed studied with one mouth, and this was the essence of their work. If so, there would be two contradictory statements. Therefore, the author of this version was compelled to retain this matter so that it speaks of the Rishonim. And he overturned the bowl, meaning that the intention refers to the doubts that they did not possess, which is a known and manifest fact. And the Gaon would not cast a drop of ink to waste.
And furthermore, and this is the more significant point, what led the Gaon to interrupt in the midst of his discourse, where he speaks about the nature of Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh's work, what he accomplished and did, in order to inform us that until the end of the days of the Rabbis no one wrote anything at all — a matter that bears no connection to what he is currently recounting? For indeed, the consistent style of a mighty author such as our Gaon necessitates that after he recounted that through the diligent efforts expended by Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh and his generation, all doubts were resolved, he should immediately explain that they did not merely accomplish this, but also arranged everything in one mouth and one tongue. And similarly, several other improvements that he made and brought to successful fruition, as the Gaon indeed proceeds to recount them one by one. [He should not] interrupt in order to inform us when the writing occurred! Truly, this matter is baseless. But what can the author of this French version do, as the Geonim of his land hold the opinion that writing commenced only in later generations? Therefore, he was compelled to confuse all these matters for us, and instead of "the days of Rabbeinu ha-Kadosh," he presumptuously wrote "the days of the Rabbis" – may his Master forgive him!
And furthermore, why "anything, anything" twice? And why "at all"? It is not the manner of a logical author such as our Gaon to burden the ear of the listener by employing different expressions within a single line. And moreover, it is the practice of a distinguished author to explicate his words with utmost precision and to remove any vestige of doubt from his pronouncements, unless it is a matter upon which his entire focus is directed for elucidation and clarification. This is not the case for a matter introduced incidentally, such as the issue of writing which was not inscribed until the end of the days of the Rabbis, which is truly an incidental remark according to the arrangement of this version's author. And if so, why "at all"? The mind of our Gaon is not presently engaged with this particular detail, nor does his natural style compel him to reinforce his words with "at all." Unless the author of this version truly intended to eradicate every mention of writing from this cherished letter, and therefore he strengthened his words by adding "at all" to buttress the contention that writing occurred only in later generations. And according to our version, everything will be clearly elucidated, and the connection of the words will be as strong as mortar; examine them with great care. And even if we do not insert the word "minhon" (from them) in the Yochasin version, nevertheless, our intention is pellucid, for the meaning refers to the books that emerged during the three years of upheaval, about which the Gaon, of blessed memory, is speaking, and not necessarily about the Oral Torah (Torah Sheb’al Peh) in general, as is the simple understanding. All the more so, since we observe in the Chatam Sofer version the words "minhon mada'm" (anything from them), which clearly attest to the truth that these words emanated from the mouth of the Gaon, of blessed memory, inasmuch as they refer to them, meaning to say, to the books that originated during the three years of upheaval. And the author of the version is suspected only of adding and subtracting concerning the matter pertaining to the idea of writing, in order to pursue his agenda; the rest he leaves as he found it, and this is clear. For even though the word "minhon" is not in Yochasin, it ought to have been used, for it was assuredly omitted, and the matter stands on its own.
