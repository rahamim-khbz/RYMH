English Translation

Regarding *Sha'arei Teshuvah*, *Siman* 187, 188, 189: This matter is common and is known, as in the Talmud and in the renowned *Poskim*, to every student of books. Among them, see above, *Siman* 184, within the aforementioned discussions. And this suffices, so that silence may not be construed as a ruling. *Siman* 185.
And regarding what you asked: 'Is the butter of non-Jews forbidden or permitted?' Since it has been established for the Rabbis that pure milk solidifies while impure milk does not solidify, it is permitted. However, the mind does not accept it. And whoever is strong in his conviction and eats it, is permitted to do so. This responsum is cited in its form in *Ha-Manhig*, *Siman* 135, and thus he states: "And that which is found in the *Teshuvot* of the *Ge'onim*: 'Regarding the butter of non-Jews, since it is established for the Rabbis that pure milk solidifies while impure milk does not solidify, whoever is strong in his conviction and accepts it for consumption, it is permitted.' One should not rely upon this, for we have proven that the reason for milk milked by a non-Jew and so forth [being forbidden] is because of a membrane-like substance, and because of impure milk. And so believe the people of the West, while the people of the East permit the butter of non-Jews and so forth." End of his words. I have shown you how he copied our responsum word for word, except that he omitted what is stated in our text: "However, the mind does not accept it." And I have shown you that "the people of the East" refers to the people of Babylonia who permit it. And *Ha-Kolbo*, in *Siman* 114, also wrote that regarding their butter, there are some among the *Ge'onim* who permitted it. And thus wrote the Rambam in Chapter 3 of the Laws of Forbidden Foods, *Halakhah* 14: "Regarding butter, some of the *Ge'onim* permitted it, for they did not issue a decree concerning butter, and the milk of a forbidden animal does not solidify. And some of the *Ge'onim* forbade it, because of the traces of milk that may remain in it." End of his words. And this is therefore what you will see, that in our responsum it is written "However, the mind does not accept it," because he knew that some *Ge'onim* forbade it, and it is clear. For if not so, there is no danger. And I found a reference to the renowned Rabbi, Mar Schnei, in the book *Chukkei Lev*, *Siman* 61, page 32b, section 6, who discussed these matters. And according to the gravity of the subject, what I have written contains a third [of the required analysis]. And see *Teshuvot Bnei Yehudah ha-Navi*, Part 3, *Siman* 10 and *Siman* 258. Rav Kohen Tzedek, of blessed memory, *Siman* 189.
Regarding bee products from which a particular substance is made: if during the rest of the year its derivatives are permitted, and there is no concern therein regarding cooked foods of non-Jews. For thus the Sages stated: 'And these are permitted for consumption: milk that a non-Jew milked and an Israelite watched him, and honey.' And we say (*Avodah Zarah* 39b): This honey, why should we be concerned about it? If because of cooked foods of non-Jews, it is eaten as it is, raw. And if because of non-Jewish concoctions, it imparts a detrimental flavor. And if because something forbidden is mixed into it, it spoils. Therefore, it is permitted from the outset. This responsum is cited in its form in *Pardes*, page 17a, without name or title. However, it appears there to be solely a collection of earlier responsa, which adds at the end of the responsum, after "it spoils": "And it is forbidden to drink from it during the days of Pesach, and its prohibition and permission are as we have commanded." End of his words. And I see that the responsum before us is primary, as it does not add these matters.
And see *Shemayim Rosh*, *Siman* 8, and what the annotators expanded upon. And who will remove the dust from the eyes of the questioner, that in the *Teshuvot* of the Ran, *Siman* 5, and the Ran himself there, concerning what they wished to falsely accuse Rabbeinu Hananel, of blessed memory, that the honey mentioned in the Mishnah *Ein Ma'amidin* and so forth, "And these are permitted for consumption: milk that a non-Jew milked and so forth," and the honey, which is the honey-like syrup of the Ishmaelites made from grape wine... Over this, the questioner and the Ran, of blessed memory, greatly feared, saying, "Heaven forbid that our Master Rabbeinu Hananel, of blessed memory, should attribute such confused matters!" See there that they elaborated. And would that this responsum before us, of Rav Kohen Tzedek, who preceded Rabbeinu Hananel, of blessed memory, be revealed to them, which explains the honey mentioned in this Mishnah as bee honey. And that is the truth, and that is the righteousness that shall be exalted. And note the textual variant before us in the Talmud in *Avodah Zarah* 39b. And see *Musaf ha-Arukh*, entry `Sar`, and note. And it is found that all is one.
