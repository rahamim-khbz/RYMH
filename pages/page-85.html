הענף הה': סיכום וחיזוק דעת הגאונים — Branch Five: Summary and Reinforcement of the Geonic View
שערי תשובה סימן קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

וקשה מפני מה לא אמרינן הלכה כבתראי? ואם לא היה להם רשות לחלוק על הגמרא, צריכין אנו לומר או כי דברי הש"ס היו נעלמים מהם או שהכניסו את עצמם בפלוגתא נגד הש"ס שלא כדין וזה זר מאד" עכ"ד וא"כ בראותו תשו' הרא"ם שאומר "שכמה דברי הגאונים נדחו שאינם על שרשי הגמ'" אמר א"כ יכול אני לחרוץ משפטי על תשובה זאת הבאה בריש שערי צדק, ולומר חלילה וכו', כלומר הרי הרא"ם עמוד גדול מעמודי ההוראה עד שהשתבח מוהרלנ"ח שתורתו תורת אמת, כי אלו היה חי הרא"ם ז"ל היה מסכים על דבריו (*) ועפ"ז צאו וראו שחלק על הגאונים וסיים וכתב, שכמה דברי הגאונים נדחו וכו'" וא"כ בהכרח תשו' זאת של רב שרירא שכותב בה "דכל החולק על דבר מדברי הגאונים, הוא כחולק על ה' ועל תורתו" ודאי הוא מזוייפה, ומי לנו גדול מהרא"ם שאומר להפך? זאת היא תכלית סמיכתו של החכם השד"ל נר"ו, ואבא בדרך ישר להראות לעיני השמש והירח, איך אלו ואלו דברי אלהים חיים, ר"ל גם דברי הרא"ם במה שיש לנו כח לחלק על הגאונים, גם דברי הגאון בתשו' זאת במה שאין לנו כח לחלוק עליהם, וכשידקדק האדם מעט בדבריהם, ימצא הדברים קרואים והולכים לתומם, דהנה הרא"ם ז"ל בפי' "שכמה דברי הגאונים נדחו מפני שאינם על שורשי הגמרא כונתו מבוארת" כשדברי הגאון אינם על שורשי הגמרא דרך משל אותה דלעיל סימן קס"ד לרב נטרונאי שנדה לא תעלה לה טבילה כי אם במים חיים, הא ודאי נדחית היא, תשובה זאת מקמי שורשי הברייתות והתוספתות, וכאשר הארכנו לעיל וכגון דא ודאי אמרינן איזה תלמיד טועה כתבה בשם ר"ן גאון וכ"כ מוהר"ם אלאשקר ז"ל בסי' כ"ז על רב נטרונאי דהכשיר הקרובים לעידי קיום עי"ש וכן כתב הראב"ד ה"ד בס' תמים דעים סי' מ"ב על תשו' רה"ג ז"ל איני יודע מי שגאה ואפשר הסופרים שבשוה וכו' עיש"ב, וכן כתב הרשב"א סי' א' קס"ו עיש"ב (מה) כי באמת אחר חתימת התלמוד לא נשאר כח ביד שום גאון לחלוק על השרשים הגדולים והקטנים

English Translation

And it is difficult: why do we not rule according to the later authorities? And if they had no right to dispute the Gemara, we are compelled to say either that the words of the Talmud were unknown to them, or that they improperly entered into a dispute against the Talmud; and this is exceedingly strange. Thus far his words. Consequently, upon seeing the Responsum of Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, who states: "that many of the words of the Geonim have been rejected because they are not founded upon the principles of the Gemara," he said: "If so, I can pass judgment on this Responsum that you brought at the beginning of *Sha'arei Tzedek*, and say, God forbid, etc." That is to say, behold, Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi is a great pillar among the pillars of Halakhic decision-making, to the extent that Our Teacher, Rabbi Levi Ibn Chaviv, praised him, saying that his Torah is a Torah of truth, for if Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, of blessed memory, were alive, he would concur with his words. And based on this, go forth and see that he disputed the Geonim and concluded by writing, "that many of the words of the Geonim have been rejected, etc." Therefore, this Responsum of Rav Sherira, in which he writes, "that anyone who disputes one of the words of the Geonim is like one who disputes the Lord and His Torah," is certainly forged. And who is greater than Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, who states the opposite? This is the ultimate authority of the Sage Rabbi Shmuel David Luzzatto, may his light shine. And I shall proceed directly to show, before the eyes of the sun and the moon, how "these and those are the words of the living God." That is to say, both the words of Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, in that we have the power to dispute the Geonim, and the words of the Gaon in that Responsum, in that we do not have the power to dispute them. And when a person carefully examines their words, he will find the matters reading and proceeding in their proper course.
And behold, Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, of blessed memory, in his interpretation "that many of the words of the Geonim have been rejected because they are not founded upon the principles of the Gemara," his intention is clear: when the words of the Gaon are not founded upon the principles of the Gemara – for example, that which is mentioned above in Section 164, concerning Rav Natronai, that a Niddah's immersion is valid only in living waters – this is certainly rejected. This Responsum is rejected before the principles of the Baraitot and Toseftot, as we have elaborated above. And in such a case, we certainly say that some mistaken student wrote it in the name of Rav Natronai Gaon. And thus wrote Our Teacher, Rabbi Moshe Alashkar, of blessed memory, in Section 27 concerning Rav Natronai, who validated relatives as witnesses for the establishment of a document. And similarly wrote Rabbi Avraham ben David in Section 4 of his book *Tamim De'im*, Section 42, concerning a Responsum of Rav Huna Gaon, of blessed memory: "I do not know who was arrogant, and perhaps the scribes corrupted it, etc." And similarly wrote Rabbi Shlomo ben Adret in Section 166 of the First Part. For truly, after the sealing of the Talmud, no Gaon retained the power to dispute the major or minor principles contained therein.
A questioner asked concerning one "who does not know the path of Halakhah nor its interpretation, but has seen from the Responsa of the Geonim, of blessed memory, and the books of Halakhic rulings. And it is not concealed from our master that the Responsa do not stand in a consistent state, particularly the older ones among them, into which corruption has entered on the part of their copyists. And furthermore, their Responsa have been attributed to others than their true authors. Also, many of the Geonim ruled in their Responsa on a single question, and thereafter retracted that ruling, or others criticized it. If a person whose primary knowledge is from these Responsa, and who does not understand which of them is correct and which is incorrect, may rule on a matter and issue a legal decision concerning it; and it is possible that the ruling from which he taught has already been retracted by its author; and one who does not understand the root principle, nor from what place in the Talmud it emanates—is he permitted to rule?"
Responsum: "Know that this man is more worthy to be permitted to rule than many people who have established themselves as arbiters of Halakhah in our time. Most of them possess not even one of these two things: namely, understanding of the Halakhah and reliance upon the opinion of the Geonim, of blessed memory. And those who presume to rule from their own investigation of Halakhah and from the strength of their diligence in the Talmud—it is they who should be prevented from doing so, for in our time there is no one worthy of it. Nor one who has attained such mastery in the wisdom of the Talmud as to rule from his own investigation without relying on the opinion of the Geonim, of blessed memory. However, one who rules from the Responsa of the Geonim and relies upon them, even if he cannot understand the Talmud, is more fitting and praiseworthy than one who thinks he knows the Talmud and relies upon himself. For even if he rules from an unsound reasoning derived from the proofs of the Geonim, of blessed memory, he is nevertheless not considered mistaken in this, for he has acted according to the directive of a great Beth Din, recognized by the multitude. And one who rules from his own investigation of Halakhah – it is possible that he thinks that Halakhah compels that ruling, yet it does not compel it, and his investigation misled him or he erred in its interpretation. And in our time, there is no one who reaches such a level in the Talmud that he can rely upon it to rule. And Responsa of some individuals have already reached me, concerning matters in which they ruled, believing they had ruled correctly and that it was as clear as the sun, yet they erred in their ruling. And they connected that matter to an inappropriate place, and learned the law from a place from which it should not be learned. And there was already a subtle distinction, and a hidden and concealed nuance, between the matter they judged and the Halakhah upon which they based that judgment, and they did not perceive it. And they took the Halakhah at its plain meaning, and did not perceive the subtle distinction, and they analogize their case to it, not perceiving the subtle distinction between them. So much so that I say that one who does not rely on himself, but depends on the Responsa of the Geonim and their rulings – which are decided Halakhot and concise and clear reasonings – is more praiseworthy than those who presume to rule from the Talmud."
How many great Halakhic rulings can be derived from this Responsum? Firstly: that in the time of Our Teacher, Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, a third generation of Rabbinic authority, corruption had already entered the Responsa of the Geonim, especially the older ones, due to the copyists. Secondly: that even from those days, an error had already occurred in attributing Responsa to others than their true authors. Thirdly: that there were already Responsa of Geonim who retracted their rulings from one Responsum to another. Fourthly, and this is the greatest of all: that Our Teacher, Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, of blessed memory, although he conceded to the questioner all that was written concerning the errors, corruptions, and damages that befell the Responsa of the Geonim, nevertheless preferred one who rules from them over one who rules from dialectical discussion of the Talmud without subsequently relying on the reasoning of the Geonim. Even if at times he rules from an unsound reasoning that the Geonim presented, ultimately he will not be considered mistaken in this, for he has acted according to the directive of a great Beth Din.
