English Translation

They established that in this matter, no definitive Halakhah can be stated by the later authorities under any circumstances, for were it otherwise, life would not be endurable! By what power are the later authorities entitled to overturn the fundamental principles based on the strength of their own analytical reasoning? And refer to *Sefer HaMachri'a*, folio 8a, concerning what is written in *Chullin*, folio 48b, which states concerning the *de'bina*, that which is attached to the meat. And regarding this, the Gaon disagreed, and in the Responsum of Rabbeinu Tzemach Gaon, they permitted it even if it is attached to the ribs. Regarding this, he concluded and wrote: "These are nothing but prophetic pronouncements seeking to uproot the method of the Talmud and the flow of the discussion, without any proof whatsoever in the world."
And if we discover some Gaon who disagreed, we should not assert that that fundamental principle was unknown to him, nor should we state, Heaven forbid, that he knew it and intentionally chose to disagree with it! Rather, since we are well-versed in the profound knowledge of that Gaon, who was a great man in Torah, we shall unequivocally state concerning that responsum which contradicts the fundamental principles of the Gemara that some erring student wrote it, as previously mentioned. And see Morenu HaRav Levi ben Chabib, folio 318a, for indeed, we have not been devoid of such erring and malicious students in any generation. And with this, the questioner's complaint, mentioned in the aforesaid citation, is instantaneously resolved. For truly, when the words of the Geonim contradict the fundamental principles of the Talmud, we are not to rely upon them. Not only this, but if the Geonim relied upon ruling according to a single Tanna, and the flow of the discussion proceeds not in accordance with that Tanna, then the words of the Geonim are rejected. See *Tashbetz*, Part 1, Siman 135, page 264, paragraph 4.
And Maimonides, of blessed memory, to whom the teachings of the Geonim were dear, and who was proficient in them, their Halakhot, and their responsa, as he has demonstrated throughout this entire work, disagreed with the Geonim in several places. This was sometimes because he discovered an explicit Baraita that contradicted their assertions, such as the case of robbers and usurers who returned their illicit gains, that we do not accept from them, from which we infer by implication that forgiveness is effective, contrary to the opinion of the Geonim. See *Hilkhot Milveh ve-Loveh*, Chapter 4, Halakhah 13. Similarly, he disagreed with them in *Hilkhot Mikva'ot*, Chapter 2, Halakhah 15, concerning its majority and its appointment. And see *Maggid Mishneh* there. And even otherwise, this is a dispute between Rashi and the Tosafot in *Sukkah*, folio 6b, regarding the opening phrase "Divrei Torah"; see there and examine well.
And he also disagreed with them when he found two textual versions, and one version was closer to reason, as is the matter of 'Do not repay me except with witnesses', as in Chapter 15 of *Hilkhot Milveh ve-Loveh*, Halakhah 2. Or, when the Yerushalmi decided between the two versions concerning the matter of 'an open opening' in *Hilkhot Ishut*, Chapter 1, Halakhah 2 and 13. And see *HaMaggid Mishneh* there.
And similarly, if the matter constituted a dispute among the Geonim, Maimonides would decide in favor of one of the parties. See *Hilkhot Ishut*, Chapter 2, Halakhah 18; and in Chapter 10 of *Hilkhot Gerushin*, Halakhah 19; and *Hilkhot Nachalot*, Chapter 4, Halakhah 6; and *Hilkhot Avadim*, Chapter 9. And see *Teshuvot HaGeonim* in *Sefer Sha'arei Tzedek*, Gate 1, Siman 17, and Gate 6, Siman 15. And likewise, he observed the customs of the Geonim and chose to rule according to the law of the Gemara, either because their custom had not spread throughout all the communities of the Diaspora (in *Hilkhot Ishut*, Chapter 14, Halakhah 14), or because it did not appear to be the custom of all the academies in Babylonia (there, Chapter 15, Halakhah 7). And even due to the doubt that their enactment might not have universally spread, he based the matter on the law of the Talmud (there, Halakhah 9).
And it is a great logical principle that in every generation, we are permitted to declare, "Are we not mere reed-cutters in a marsh?" and we are capable of disagreeing with the Geonim and with the Rabbinan Savora'im when we possess demonstrative proofs from the Talmud. As we wrote above, in the name of Maran Morenu HaRav Yosef Kolon, of blessed memory, in *Teshuvot Avkat Rokhel*, Siman 155. And upon this foundation rest the words of Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, of blessed memory, mentioned above, when he states: "For many of the pronouncements of the Geonim have been rejected when they are not based on the fundamental principles of the Gemara." And this is clearer than an egg in *kutchah*. Conversely, when the pronouncements of the Geonim do not contradict the Talmud, and they establish a new law, or they provide an interpretation in the Talmud contrary to our reasoning, and they do not bring proof for their words—nevertheless, since we have not found anything contrary to their words in the Talmud, we are not permitted to disagree with them. And this is what the Radbaz, Part 2, folio 86, Siman 559, states: "This is the essence of the matter: any responsum of the Geonim that does not explicitly contradict the Talmud, we rely upon it, even if his name is not signed on it. And any responsum that contradicts the Talmud or one of the principles transmitted to us, we do not rely upon it."
And refer to what is written above, Siman 9, and examine there, for it is entirely aligned with all that has been stated. And see *Teshuvot Binyamin Ze'ev*, Part 1, Siman 32, folio 22a, regarding the well-known dispute concerning a claim in a written document, whether one can argue 'I have paid' or not. Regarding this, he wrote in the name of the Rashba, stating that "if we had found this explicitly stated by the masters of the Gemara, it would be proper to wonder and inquire: 'Why?' All the more so now, when there is no fundamental principle in the Gemara, and it is not stated based on logical reasoning alone." And although the *Tashbetz* agrees with the Rashba in all his proofs, stating that the words of the Rashba are indeed words of reason, he nevertheless concluded there in two places and said: "And regarding the practical matter about which you inquired, it is impossible to disagree with the Geonim." See there. Thus, even in a matter that contradicts our own reasoning, as long as we do not possess demonstrative proof from the Gemara contradicting the Geonim, it is impossible to disagree with them. And this is a fundamental principle established for the entire Torah, observed by all the Rishonim who preceded us, until the generation of Maran HaKadosh. In our time, since we have descended many thousands of levels, all the more so we cannot rely on our own casuistry to derive Halakhot, unless we search among the very latest authorities. And I have already written sufficiently about this in *Kuntresei Kin'at Tzion* (folio 16a), within an enclosed essay. And refer to *Teshuvot Besamim Rosh*, Siman 158, at the beginning of the responsum.
