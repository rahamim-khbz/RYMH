English Translation

until he reached the generation of Our Holy Rabbi, to whom the *Gaon* particularly devoted his attention. After he, incidentally, made known the wisdom of Rabbi Meir, and his eloquence and his authority—for even to this he had great need—he there related that Rabbi Shimon ben Gamliel raised his beloved son, Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, of blessed memory, for the study of Torah. He also related who his teachers were, and thereafter he related that he reigned after his father, and who the Sages of his generation were, and who his disciples were, stating: "And other disciples, such as Rabbi Chiyya, and so forth..." And they (meaning to say, Rabbi [Yehudah HaNasi] and his colleagues and his disciples) disseminated much Torah. And in all those years, all the *Halakhot* that had been pending in the study house subsequent to the great loss that occurred with the destruction of the Temple, and the doubts that arose for them in those disturbances, and all the disagreements that arose in these three generations – the *Halakhah* was decided concerning them, and the views of individuals and the views of the majority became known among them. After the Rabbis exerted themselves greatly concerning them, and scrutinized them with meticulous precision, and carefully examined all the traditions and *Mishnayot* to rectify them, and it was not that they added to the statements of the predecessors, the Men of the Great Assembly, but rather they labored with great effort and scrutinized with significant rigor, until they concluded what those early authorities had said, and what they had done, until all their doubts were resolved. And not one of the early authorities had written anything thereof until the end of the days of Our Holy Rabbi.
In these remarks, our *Gaon* already began to reach his desired objective: to instruct the inquirer with complete clarity how what he told him at the beginning of his words, "all these things are the words of the predecessors," is true and firm. For he demonstrates to him demonstrably how Rabbi [Yehudah HaNasi] and his colleagues and his disciples, in their arrangement of the *Mishnah*, did not add to the statements of the predecessors, the Men of the Great Assembly, but rather exerted great efforts and scrutinized with precision to establish the tradition according to the *Halakhah*. Until, through the abundance of their labor and their dialectical argumentation, they came to say what the early authorities had said, meaning to say, to rule in matters of *Halakhah* as the early authorities had ruled, and to perform the commandments and the like as the early authorities had performed them, without addition and without diminution. And the distinction is that the early authorities had stated those very laws without exerting any effort, while the later authorities, indeed, arrived at all that the early authorities had stated, but with immense toil. And after they succeeded in clarifying the views and practices of the early authorities, they established them in the *Mishnah* as *Halakhah* for generations. And this is what is meant by: "but rather they labored, and so forth, until they concluded what those early authorities had said, and what they had done, until all their doubts were resolved."
Meaning to say, that through their labor they arrived at the essence of the law and the essence of the practice of the early authorities, for through the doubts that arose in the three generations of tumult, doubts arose concerning the branches of the laws themselves, whether to prohibit or to permit. And through the toil of meticulous scrutiny, they were resolved, and they reverted to ruling (meaning, stating) and acting in accordance with all that the early authorities had ruled, stated, and done. And after the *Gaon* clarified this matter with no less clarity than the sun at midday, it was then felt that one might say, "If so, what new contribution did Our Holy Rabbi and his loving company make?" To this he responds, "And not one of the early authorities had written anything thereof until the end of the days of Our Holy Rabbi."
The meaning is: This is the novelty that he introduced, inasmuch as concerning all those doubts that arose in the branches of the laws and their sub-branches—for example, whether one washes hands and then sweeps the house, or vice-versa—concerning all this and similar matters, not a single one of the early authorities had written anything. And the reason for these matters is: that among the early authorities, no such doubt ever arose, for even the discussions of Abaye and Rava were entirely clear to the early authorities, as stated above, so how could a doubt have arisen? And if the early ones among the later authorities, since they lived in the three generations of tumult, did not have the opportune time to serve their masters adequately, all the more so were they unable to attend to and clarify these matters, until Our Holy Rabbi came, and he and his colleagues and his disciples toiled, and through this all matters were clarified as clearly as a garment, both that which was undisputed and that which was the reverse, and similar cases. And at the end of his days, they were written in a book, meaning to say, all the matters that required clarification, together with all the matters that had already been clarified and recorded in scrolls, and the remembrance of traditions and complete tractates that were in the hands of the great ones of the generations, until a complete compilation was formed, as the *Gaon* will explain this matter several times further on.
