ומאלה אתה כותב נפקא דבר והופכו, כמו שדרשת "אלה אתה כותב" א"א לפרשה על מצות כתיבת ס"ת, כי אם על מצות אמירתן מתוך הספר, ואיסורן בע"פ, כן "ואי אתה כותב הלכות" דדרשינן מניה אינו על איסור כתיבה, כי אם על איסור קריאתן מתוך הכתב, וכאשר הארכתי לעיל, וכן הך "דכותבי הלכות כשורפי תורה" לא הזכירו הגאון ז"ל, אפשר דס"ל דלאו הלכה הוא, ואפשר והוא האמת כ"ש לעיל דקאי על כותבי הלכות שמלאכתן בכך, כמו כותבי ספרים ומגילות לאחרים, כי באופן כזה ודאי הם כשורפי תורה, שע"י רוב מציאותן יבואו מן ההתר אל האיסור, כי לאו כ"ע רבנן דהוו זהירין שלא ללמוד מתוך הכתב, וע"ז אין ספק שהיו הכתיבות חמדות גנוזות ביד גדולי הדור, וראשי ישיבות, כי יראת ה' היא אוצרם, ומי יאמר לא כן לגאונים בדורם כרבינו שרירא ובנו דהוו נהירין להו שבילי דתלמודא יותר משבילי דנהרדעה. ומי לנו בקיאים בכל עניני הראשונים בקיאות נהיר ובהיר בשחקים אשר ראוי לסמוך עליו יותר מהם? יאמר לי הקורא החפשי המבקש דרך סלולה מחסדו הנעים! הגע עצמך שגאוננו ר"ש ובנו מצאו גמרות, והלכות, ומשניות כתובים מימות נביאים ראשונים, כי אינו דבר רחוק אלא קרוב למאד שעדיין התורה היתה שוקטת על שמריה בבבל, וראשי סנהדראות וגאוניה, לא הורקו מכלי אל כלי ובגולה לא הלכו, וברי להו לאב ולבן שראו את החדש שהיה כתיבתן וחתימתן של ראשונים, כמו בימינו שיש לנו כתיבתן וחתימתן של ראשונים (כה) וכמו שבאמת מעידין בגודלן בתשו' זאת שבשערי תשובה דאייתין עלה לעיקר מטרין, ובפירושא קאמרי שלאחר שנלב"ע חד מהגאונים מצאו באמתחתו "שיתא סדרי משנה דאתגניזו מיומי דהלל" וכבר בתחילת דברינו כתבנו עליה הנפק דבתלתא הויא חזקה, א"כ נמצא דגאוננו ז"ל ובנו חמודו הוחזקו לטעון טענת ברי שהיו חמדות גנוזות מימות תנאים ראשונים, וכיוצא בדבר מעיד לקמן (יוחסין ר"א דפ"ח ע"א) "שמענו מן הראשונים וראינו כתוב בספרי זכרוניהם" (כו) ואחר כל ידיעות בריאות וטובות שהיו לגאוננו ובנו ז"ל רואים סוגיאות הללו וכיוצא בהם דלפי פשטן מבורר יוצא איסורא דרביע על כותבי הלכות, עד דהוו כשורפי תורה, הנה נמצאו הגאונים כמי שנתנום בין ב' חרבות חדות, התלמוד קורא בגרון לאסור איסר לכתוב אפי' אות אחת (לפי' פשט פירש"י בכל התלמוד) וכנגד זה רואים מעשה רב ועדיו בחותמיו זכין לו שהיו מותרין לכתוב ולחבר, א"כ בהכרח יפרשו כל הסוגיאות הנז"ל כמו שביארנום מרישא דסדרא, והוא שהאיסור הוא דוקא ללמוד מתוכן, אבל לכותבם כדי לסדרם ולתרצם, בלשון ופה אחד כדי להיות זהירין למגרסינהו ע"פ, ועיקר הכתבי יד יהיו גנוזים ועומדים ביד השרידים, הא ודאי פטור ומותר, וגם דבריו פה בתשו' שלפנינו הנז"ל באומרו: "שיתא סדרי משנה דאתגניזו מיומי הלל" מוכח להדיא כן דלעולם היתה כתיבה, אבל בתנאי שתהיה גנוזה ביד השרידים, ולמה

English Translation

And from this you derive a matter and its opposite. Just as you expounded "These you shall write" cannot be interpreted as referring to the commandment to write a Sefer Torah, but rather to the commandment to recite them from the book, and their prohibition by oral tradition; so too, "And you shall not write Halakhot," from which we derive, does not refer to the prohibition of writing, but rather to the prohibition of reading them from a written text, as I have expounded at length above. And similarly, the dictum, "Those who write Halakhot are like those who burn the Torah," the Gaon, of blessed memory, did not mention it. It is possible that he held it was not a Halakhah. And it is possible, and this is the truth, as stated above, that it refers to those who write Halakhot professionally, like scribes who write Sefarim and Megillot for others. For in such a manner, they are certainly like those who burn the Torah, as through their widespread availability, people will move from the permissible to the prohibited, since not everyone is a Rabbinic scholar who is scrupulous not to learn from written texts. And regarding this, there is no doubt that these writings were hidden treasures in the hands of the great scholars of the generation and the heads of the Yeshivot, for the fear of God is their treasure. And who would say otherwise concerning the Geonim of their generation, such as Rabbenu Sherira and his son, for whom the paths of the Talmud were clearer than the paths of Nehardea?
And who among us is expert in all matters of the Rishonim with an expertise so clear and shining in the heavens, upon whom it is more fitting to rely than upon them? Let the unconstrained reader who seeks a clear path, through his pleasant kindness, tell me! Imagine that our Gaon, Rabbenu Sherira, and his son, found Gemarot, Halakhot, and Mishnayot written from the days of the early Prophets. For it is not a distant matter, but very close, that the Torah was still settled upon its lees in Babylonia, and the heads of the Sanhedrins and its Geonim were not poured from vessel to vessel and did not go into exile. And it is clear to the father and son that they saw the novelty that was the writing and sealing of the Rishonim, just as in our days we have the writing and sealing of the Rishonim. And as they indeed testify to their greatness in this Responsum, which we brought from Sha’arei Teshuvah as a primary source, they explicitly state that after one of the Geonim passed away, they found in his possession "Six Orders of Mishnah that were hidden since the days of Hillel." And we already wrote at the beginning of our words regarding the inference that a repeated instance constitutes a presumption. Thus, it is found that our Gaon, of blessed memory, and his beloved son, were presumed to assert with certainty that these were hidden treasures from the days of the early Tanna'im. And similarly, it is testified further on, "We heard from the Rishonim and saw it written in their books of remembrance." And after all the sound and good knowledge that our Gaon, of blessed memory, and his son possessed, they saw these sugyot and similar ones, whose plain meaning clearly yields a severe prohibition against those who write Halakhot, to the extent that they are like those who burn the Torah. Behold, the Geonim found themselves as if placed between two sharp swords. The Talmud cries out aloud to prohibit even writing a single letter (according to Rashi’s plain interpretation throughout the Talmud), and against this, we see the widespread practice, and its witnesses, with their seals, testifying that it was permissible to write and to compose. Therefore, they must necessarily interpret all the aforementioned sugyot as we explained them from the beginning of the order. This means that the prohibition is specifically to learn from them, but to write them in order to arrange and reconcile them, with one voice and one mouth, so that those who study them orally may be scrupulous. And the main handwritten copies shall be hidden and remain in the hands of the select few. This is certainly exempt and permissible. And also his words here in the Responsum before us, mentioned above, when he states: "Six Orders of Mishnah that were hidden since the days of Hillel" explicitly prove that writing always existed, but on condition that it be hidden in the hands of the select few. And why would he say:
And I, the humblest of my thousands, have in my possession, from the legacy of my esteemed grandfather, the master of *Chikrei Lev*, of blessed memory, the handwriting of our Gaon, Rabbi Yosef Caro, of blessed memory; and Rabbi Yosef Taitazak; and the Mabbit (Rabbi Moshe Miterani); and Rabbi Shmuel de Medina; and Rabbi Yosef Miterani; and Rabbi Yisrael Benjamin; and Rabbi Yoshiyahu Pinto; and the Kenesset HaGedolah; and Rabbi Yosef Iskapa, the author of *Rosh Yosef*; and Rabbi Meir Albilda, of blessed memory. Some of these are signed from nearly three hundred years ago. And it is clear to me that these are their signatures, inasmuch as they came to us through inheritance from father to son, from my grandfather, Rabbi Eliyahu Chazan, the colleague of Rabbi Yosef Miterani, of blessed memory. And he bequeathed them to my great-grandfather, his son, the author of *Ein Yehosef*, a commentary on *Bava Metzia*, the colleague of the Kenesset HaGedolah. And he bequeathed them to his son, Rabbi Shnot Chayyim. And Rabbi Shnot Chayyim to his son, Rabbi Chozeh Dor. And Rabbi Chozeh Dor to his son, Rabbi Chayyim, of blessed memory. And he to his son, the famous Gaon, the author of *Chikrei Lev*. And he to my father, the Gaon, Rabbi Eliezer HaGadol, of blessed memory, in a special will worded thus: "And my books to my wise son Eliezer." And my father, of blessed memory, to me, the writer who speaks in my lifetime. And may God grant me the merit to bequeath them, along with all my books, to my young son, Yosef Yafah, may he live long, whose name is like the name of his teachers and grandfathers, the Gaon, the author of *Ein Yehosef*, and the Gaon, the author of *Chikrei Lev*. So may God, Master of Mercies, declare. And if one is so, what is so astonishing about the Gaon, the chain of lineage of Rabbenu Sherira, of blessed memory, if his eye saw a hidden treasure from the days of Hillel, after the Torah had settled upon its lees in Babylonia, as mentioned above.
Here, truth fell as if by chance, and it happened that in the text of *Chazon Nachum*, page 38, he omitted "and we saw it written" and copied it as follows: "We heard from the Rishonim) and in their books of remembrance." This was out of fear that the language of writing here might also become a stumbling block and an obstacle to his established principle that writing never existed for the Oral Torah. And the foolish copyist did not know that it is no matter at all, either because the nature of this writing is a recounting of the generations from the beginning, and is not subject to prohibition, or because their books of remembrance do not refer to Mar bar Rav Ashi and his generation, but rather to the Rishonim who preceded our Geonim, who permitted writing, and also wrote down this very remembrance. But from Heaven, they fought with the copyist to retrieve the stolen item from his hand. Blessed is He who illuminates the eyes of the wise, and "let not the snare of the cunning entrap you," said the Merciful One in bd 22.
