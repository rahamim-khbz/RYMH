נוסח עברי

לומר על הכתיבה של מי היא, העיד שמצאה בי ר' חייא שהוא אוצר הברייתות הגדולות והנאמנות, וכל מה שנמצא בישיבת ר' חייא הוחזק למקובל ואמתי, ולפי מה שאני זוכר שבג' מקומות שהובא זה, הובא בלשון זה ומאופן השני הלזה, ירצה דינים ומאמרים שלא היו קבועים בגוף המשניות והברייתות,
הוא כל מה שתמצא שמוכרחים להשמיע שם כתיבה כמו "כתוב אפנקסא דלוי, "כתוב אפנקסיה דזעירי כתוב אפנקסיה דריב"ל בשבת דקנ"ו, וכן "מדכתב אפנקסיה דאילפא" במנחות ד"ע ע"א, בכל אלו ידועים, שהיו מקבלים גדולים, ואוצר הקבלה בידם, וכיון שנתקיים דהיו פנקסיהם של גדולי הדורות הללו די והותר אפי' שלא נשמעה מפיו, וכן הביא דשלח רבין באגרתיה כתובות דמ"ט, וב"ב דקל"ט, וכן העיד ר' יעקב בר אחא בסנהדרין דז"ן ע"ב דהוה כתיב בספרא דאגדתא דבי רב וכו' וכיון דאתברר לו דהוא ספרא דאגדתא דבי רב, דין קיומיה ואתקיים, וכן רבות, וכולם הם מפני שלא נשמעו מפי המקבלים, וגם לא נמצאו כתובים באוצרו הקבלה הם המשניות והברייתות, והתוספתות, והוכרחו לגלות בית הסתרים דרבנן ולהוציא מפיהם כתיבה, וגם זה היה מן השמים להחיותינו כיום הזה, בבירור ענין זה על מתכונתו
ודבר אחד לבד, נשאר להעירך בו קורא נעים! ואסגור הענף הלזה בו, ראה פלא עצום! כמה נדחק רש"י בכל המקומות, לקיים יסודו כאשר הראיתיך בכל הילוך ענין זה מריש ועד כען, ועוד מי יודע כמה, אבל שמע דבר זה האחרון, וגם בעיניך יפלא! הנה, בחולין ד"ס ע"ב, כתוב בזה הלשון: א"ל ר"ח לרב תחליפא ב"א, זיל כתוב קניגי ובליסטרי באגדתיך, וכן כתוב ארנוקי באגדתיך, שפשט הדברים אין ספק שהוא כך: כתוב חידוש זה או שמות אלו בספר אגדתא שלך, א"כ יוצא שיש ספר אגדה כתוב, לזה פי' רש"י "אגדה זו כתוב שיש בה תיבות יפות ולשון צח" ע"כ. כלומר זה הותר לך לכתוב, ולא דבר אחר, והוא פלא ! !
עוד דבר קטון אחבר פה, והוא, שבהיותי בווין, קניתי לי' ס' אגרת בקורת להגאון הצ"ח ז"ל (*) שאני לא ראיתיהו מעולם (עיין לעיל סי' ט"ל) ונדפס שנית עם הגהות מרב אחד נר"ו, ושם ראיתי שמביא מ"ש החכם השד"ל באהב גר שהתרגום לא נכתב, מדאית הירושלמי, מושיט תרגומא מגו ספרא, וגם מ"ש בכרם חמד, מראית לא יקרא פרשה ולא יתרגם, והשיב לו הרב המגיה שם, דכוונת הירוש' לא על איסור הכתיבה, כי אם על איסור הלמוד מתוך הספר, וכן ההיא דב"מ פי' כאשר פירשנו, בשם הריטב"א, ולא הזכירו, ואני עמדתי משתומם, איך בדחיות קלות כמו אלה, דחה דברי החכם השד"ל נר"ו, אשר מלא את ידו בראיות ברורות מכמה מקומות וכמה גדולים קיימי

English Translation

To state whose writing it is, he testified that Rabbi Chiya possessed it, and he was the repository of the great and reliable Baraitot. And everything found in the academy of Rabbi Chiya was considered accepted and true. And as I recall, in three places where this was cited, it was cited with this formulation and in this other manner, meaning that legal rulings (Halakhot) and sayings (Ma'amarim) that were not fixed in the body of the Mishnayot and Baraitot are everything you find where it is necessary to mention writing, such as, "It is written in Levi's notebook," "It is written in Ze'iri's notebook," "It is written in Reish Lakish's notebook" in Tractate Shabbat, page 156b, and likewise, "It is written in Ilfa's notebook" in Tractate Menachot, page 70a. In all these instances, it is known that they were great recipients of tradition, and the treasury of tradition was in their hands. And since it was established that these were the notebooks of the great sages of those generations, this was sufficient and more than sufficient, even if it was not heard from their own mouths. And similarly, he brought proof that Ravin sent in his epistle, Tractate Ketubot, page 49a, and Tractate Bava Batra, page 139b. And similarly, Rabbi Ya'akov bar Acha testified in Tractate Sanhedrin, page 75b, that it was written in the aggadic book of the academy of Rav, etcetera. And since it was clarified to him that it was the aggadic book of the academy of Rav, its establishment was accepted and confirmed. And there are many such cases, all of them because they were not heard directly from the tradition-bearers, nor were they found written in the treasury of tradition, which consists of the Mishnayot, the Baraitot, and the Toseftot. And they were compelled to reveal the hidden chambers of the Sages and to bring forth writing from them. And this too was from Heaven, to keep us alive to this very day, by clarifying this matter according to its structure. And regarding one point, it remains to enlighten you, pleasant reader! And with this, I shall close this chapter.
See the tremendous wonder, how Rashi strained in all places to uphold his fundamental principle, as I have shown you throughout the course of this matter from beginning to end, and who knows how much more. But hear this last point, and it too will seem wondrous in your eyes! Behold, in Tractate Chullin, page 60b, it is written in this language: Rabbi Chiya said to Rav Tachlifa bar Abdimi, "Go write *kenigi* and *balisteri* in your Aggadah," and similarly, "write *arnuki* in your Aggadah." The plain meaning of these words is undoubtedly thus: "Write this novelty or these names in your book of Aggadah." If so, it emerges that there was a written book of Aggadah. To this, Rashi explained: "This Aggadah is written, in which there are beautiful words and clear language." That is to say, "This is permitted for you to write, and nothing else," and this is astounding!
A small point I shall append here is that when I was in Vienna, I acquired the book *Iggeret Bikoret* by the Gaon Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Chajes, of blessed memory, which I had never seen before (see above, section 39), and it was reprinted with annotations by another Rabbi, may he live for good days. And there I saw that he cites what the Sage Shadal wrote in *Ohev Ger*, that the Targum was not written, based on the Jerusalem Talmud, which states, "He hands the Targum from a book," and also what is written in *Kerem Chemed*, that "He does not read the Torah portion and does not translate." And the annotating Rabbi there responded to him that the intent of the Jerusalem Talmud is not regarding the prohibition of writing, but rather the prohibition of learning from the book; and similarly that passage in Tractate Bava Metzia, chapter 5, as we explained in the name of the Ritba, but he did not mention him. And I stood astonished. It is not with such facile rejections that one can rebut the words of the Sage Shadal, may he live for good days, who fortified his position with clear proofs from several sources and with whom many great sages concurred in his reasoning, as he stated on page 254: "In any case, all this indicates that both the chains of tradition and the intricate discussions were already ordered before them, yet with all this, the books of the Talmud were not yet in the hands of every person, for then they would no longer need mnemonic signs and we would just bring a Torah scroll and see."
See if he does not truly prophesy regarding what has been said and discussed with a thousand irrefutable proofs. And the more he delves into the depth of this matter, pearls and precious stones continue to fall from his lips in clarification of this matter. For you see there, in that same part, page 128, that he cited what he himself wrote in *Toldot Rabbeinu Chiya*, "that the intention of the interpretation 'from Talmud to Talmud' refers only to the method of study there, and not to the written books in the two lands of the Talmudists," [and further] "they were not yet in the world in the days of the Amora'im, as I have proven elsewhere." Thus far his words. And one who writes "they were not yet in the world," how can he then write "the books of the Talmud were not yet in the hands of every person"? For infer from this that they were in the world, but not in the hands of every person, but rather hidden in the possession of the heads of the Sanhedrins? Unless you say, as I wrote, that the more he delved into the depth of this matter, the more it became clear to him that writing existed in the world, but it was not in the hands of every person. And one cannot fault a great man of his stature in any way, for truly, everything he touched upon regarding this mighty issue in all these places was a matter mentioned incidentally, as the reader will see in all his words in their respective places and contexts.
In contrast to this, our Master and the light of our eyes, Rashi, of blessed memory, is a lion crouching, standing at the crossroads before the inquirer, leaving no way to diverge. And therefore, were it not for the breadth of his work and its vast scope, he would not have been able to write incidentally what he wrote on page 140, previously mentioned. And the profundity of his sharpness and his erudition enabled him to ascertain the clear truth, even without standing upon it in its full form and stature. May his name endure forever; before the sun, may his name be established, and may all who uphold the Torah be blessed through him, as it is written.
And after some time, I saw him again in *Kerem Chemed*, Part 3, page 48, where he leans towards Rashi's view that the Talmud was not written until the time of the Savora'im, and all the proofs he brought within parentheses are precisely those given above in the name of the Gaon Rabbi Moshe Zacuto, of blessed memory. And he also maintains that the Aggadot were written in an earlier period, in accordance with the opinion of the Gaon Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Chajes, may he live for good days, whose words were cited above in the first chapter we are discussing. And I deduced that since it is only signed with three stars, this is the reason it escaped my notice when I perused all parts of *Kerem Chemed* upon their arrival. Be that as it may, even this is not proof that he wrote this before what he wrote in Part 6, page 54, and mentioned at length. And I am confident that when he sees all our proofs in their full form and stature, he will revert to rule according to the opinion of the Geonim. And it is a great Mitzvah that he should clarify his exalted opinion, like a critic who rules on the Halakhah. And those who come after us will build precisely, and the land shall be tranquil. Alas for a good finding that was lost from the world! Now here, in the crying notebook.
