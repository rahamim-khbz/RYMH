הענף הב': ביאור אגרת רב שרירא גאון — Branch Two: Elucidation of Rav Sherira Gaon's Epistle
שערי תשובה סי' קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

בענף הה', ועל זה ועל כיוצא בזה, חתימת התלמוד נקרא "סוף הוראה" ר"ל שיש ביד החכמים האחרונים לחלק על הראשונים שקדמום בראיות מופתיות מן התלמוד, אבל לא על למעלה מזה, שכבר התלמוד ופלפולו ורוב עוביו ורוב הקיפו נתקבל באומה לתדיר ומקודש, ובו נהגה יומם ולילה, וממנו תורה והוראה יוצאה לכל ישראל (כג) ואחר שהגאון בירר תשובתו, כשמלה בת מאה אלף יריעות, והשתדל בכל מאמצי כחו להוציא מלבן של שואלין דהתלמוד אינו תורה חדשה, שלא היתה לראשונים, כי באמת הוא קבלה ככל יסודי התורה והלכות דעות הכתובים בו, ובירר וזיקק הדברים להראות לכל באי עולם במופתים חותכים שבכל דור ודור מטבע הענין היו מוכרחים לסדר ולקבוע גמרהון, מפני שבכל דור ודור לפי מיעוט הלב, היה מוסיף להתרחב התלמוד והולך עד זמן חתימת התלמוד, א"כ מזה ודאי יוצא מבורר כמושכלות ראשונות, דא"א בשום אופן בלי כתיבה, וכמו שכן נראה ג"כ מדברי השואלים שכבר היו יודעים שנכתבו, אבל שאול שאלו "כיצד נכתבו" על אופן הסידור ועל הזמן, ואם באמת עתה כדבריהם כן הוא, כי מצד טבע הענין א"א בלאו הכי: ר"ל שא"א למסדר דברים וקובעם לדורות מבלי שישתמש במלאכת הכתיבה, ואפי' לרשום ראשי מילין זה אחר זה, כמו שאנו עושים בדרשות כדי לדרוש אותם בקהל בע"פ, וזה לא יכול הגאון להכחיש להם, ובפרט שבכל דבריו, ובכל מרוצת התשו' כבר הודה בפירוש שבכל דור ודור נכתבו ונסדרו, וסברא גדולה היא שכך נפל הענין והוא: שאחרי שראו דורות הבאים אחרי רבי זיע"א מלאכתו החמודה בחיבור המשניות, כבר הוקל להם הענין לעשות בכל המתחדש, כדמות שעשה רבינו הקדוש בש"ס משנה והוא טבע קיים בכל דורות האחרונים, להדמות לראשונים, ודבר זה אינו צריך ראיות (ועיין הרמב"ם בהקדמתו לס' זרעים בד"ה אחר כן דברים כהויתן) וגם כבר ביררנו בהערות דכל לשון כתיבה שיש באגרת הגאון ז"ל הכי יצאה מקולמוסו מבלי שום ריח ספק, ונוסח בעל ח"מ במעילה קאי, א"כ נמצא שגאוננו ז"ל כותב הנפק על ס' השואלים דהיתה כתיבה בעולם, ומעולם לא נאסרה בשום

English Translation

As for branch E, and regarding this and similar matters, the conclusion of the Talmud is called "the end of instruction," that is to say, the later Sages have the authority to dispute with the earlier Sages who preceded them, based on conclusive proofs from the Talmud. But not beyond this, for the Talmud, with its casuistry, its great depth, and its comprehensive scope, has already been accepted by the nation as eternal and sacred, and in it, they conduct themselves day and night, and from it, Torah and instruction issue forth to all Israel.
And after the Gaon clarified his Teshuvah, like a garment of a hundred thousand sheets, and strove with all his might to disabuse the questioners of the notion that the Talmud is a new Torah, which was not known to the earlier Sages. For, in truth, it is a tradition like all the fundamental principles of the Torah and the Halakhot of doctrines written therein. And he clarified and refined the matters to show all mankind with conclusive proofs that in every generation, by the very nature of the matter, they were compelled to arrange and establish their Gemara, because in every generation, due to the diminished intellect, the Talmud continued to expand until the time of its conclusion. Therefore, from this, it is certainly clear, like a self-evident truth, that it is impossible in any way without writing. And this also appears from the words of the questioners, who already knew that it was written, but their query was "how were they written?" concerning the method of arrangement and the time. And if, indeed, it is now as they say, for by the nature of the matter it is impossible otherwise: that is to say, it is impossible to arrange matters and establish them for generations without utilizing the craft of writing, even merely to record key phrases one after another, as we do in homilies to deliver them orally to the congregation.
And the Gaon could not deny this to them, particularly since in all his words, and throughout the course of the Teshuvah, he already explicitly admitted that in every generation, they were written and arranged. And it is a weighty reasoning that the matter transpired thus: that after the generations following Rabbi, of blessed eternal memory, observed his cherished work in the compilation of the Mishnayot, it became easier for them to do with all new developments, in the manner that Our Holy Rabbi did with the Six Orders of the Mishnah. And this is an enduring nature in all later generations, to emulate the earlier Sages, and this matter requires no proofs.
And we have also already clarified in the notes that every expression of writing found in the letter of the Gaon, of blessed memory, emanated from his pen without any trace of doubt. And the position of the author of *Choshen Mishpat* regarding *Me'ilah* holds true. Thus, it is found that our Gaon, of blessed memory, writes the refutation against the questioners, that writing existed in the world, and was never forbidden in any way.
And the summation of these words has already issued from the mouths of two Rabbis in Zion, page 108 and page 109, except that the questioner devotes all his strength to raising difficulties, and to leaving a desolate world even without a third of settlement. And concerning this, he once again aligns himself with those who adhere, and there on page 108, he wrote thus: "The questioner said: Ultimately, it is difficult to determine in whose hands lay the authority to close the gates of instruction." Thus far his words. Indeed, I wonder! In whose hands lay the authority? In the hands of those who closed the gates of instruction in the Land of Israel during the time of the descent to Babylonia, which was not a short time before the conclusion of the Talmud. And this was the bitter and swift persecution, which we have not lacked in every generation. And therefore, when persecution reached Babylonia as well, it was the cause for the conclusion of the Talmud and the sealing of the era of instruction. That is to say, the sealing of that ancient authority and that style. And there remained to us the power to compare one matter to another in any nascent doubt, to liken it to an explicit source found in the Talmud. But anything that innovates after this is not a sealed Torah, since a later authority may dispute an earlier one who preceded him, with other conclusive proofs that he will bring from the Talmud itself, as was clarified. And this was the strength of the Rif, the Rambam, and all the great earlier Sages after the conclusion of the Talmud. And in this lay the great strength of the Rashba and his contemporaries.
And what has the chain of tradition to do with this, that the questioner should approach us there on page 109 with such great force? We seek an open heart to understand matters in their proper form, but it is not, for God has afflicted him with a doubling of visible evils and emaciation of the flesh. And what has the chain of tradition to do with this? They are! And this is the law, and this is the very reason, that is to say, the abundance of troubles and the paucity of wisdom in our generation, and in the generation before us, and before the generation before us, that was the cause that neither to us nor to them was it given to rule on any dispute in the Gemara, for it has already been rectified by the rulings of the earlier and later Sages.
The sum of the matter is that everything that the two aforementioned Torah scholars replied on this matter there in Zion is like two drops of pearls, upon which nothing can be added. For we do not come by reason of the chain of tradition, but by reason of the troubles of the heart. On the contrary, the proof from *Ibbuyr* that the questioner brought will prove that the tradition was not interrupted, even though it has no root or source in the entire Six Orders as the questioner claims. We follow it: "And we have a tradition that this order was established by Hillel, the son of the son of Our Holy Rabbi, but I do not know the source for this matter." Thus are the words of the Rashba. And similarly the Tashbatz, Part I, Responsum 136, regarding Hillel the last, who lived in the days of Abbaye and Rava, and was also an Amora. And he is our established arranger, as it is traditionally received by us, and as the Ramban wrote in his *Sefer ha-Mitzvot*.
And like these are many traditions, one and nine, and we lean upon them, like the blind man who leans upon the healthy-sighted one. And regarding the matter of the Rabbanan Savorai that the questioner brought in his difficulty: "How did the Rabbanan Savorai permit themselves to introduce new matters into the body of the Talmud?" Is this not mere emptiness? Firstly, the matters they introduced were nothing but reasonings and explanations, and they would be written, and it is known and clear how many there are, as our Gaon wrote in this letter, which we are engaged in explaining. Therefore, it is as if they added nothing. Moreover, what has straw to do with grain? They, who were close to the instruction, knew that they had permission for those matters which they added, and surely, they had a tradition, since until their days they studied the Talmud orally, as will be discussed below in branches C and D. And these are among the matters which are neither useful nor saving, and they are not worthy of being recorded in a book with ink, unless by one who has not served Torah scholars, nor trod the path of Talmudic give-and-take, like this questioner, as is evident from his writing. May the pleasant reader grant of his goodness and be abundant in forgiveness if the matter compelled us to depart from the constructive in things pertaining to our intention in explaining the Teshuvah of our Gaon.
