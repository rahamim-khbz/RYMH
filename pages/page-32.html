English Translation

Go forth and consider other Sages who were solely Amoraim, such as [so-and-so], etcetera, and Rabbah bar Avuha our elder, etcetera, Chanina, etcetera, and after their generation, Rav Nachman, etcetera, etcetera, and thereafter, and thereafter, were Rav Abaye and Rava. And apostasy proliferated in the Land of Israel, and instruction diminished there, and those who were there, from among the Babylonians, descended, such as Ravin and Rav Dimi, and all the descenders who came hither.
And after our Gaon of blessed memory had completed his excellent, clear, and necessary explanations, which were well-connected to the matter he was discussing, he returned to the point from which he had departed, to inform us that in every generation the Talmud was extended, and they arranged it with one mouth and one tongue, as it is before us, whether incomplete or excessive. And this is what he states:
Furthermore, this is what he says: "And in every generation of those generations, etc., etc., another generation came and intellectual capacity diminished. And those matters that were simple for the earlier ones, and which they had explained to their students, and were like explanations that did not need to be studied and fixed in the Gemara, now became, in that generation, matters of doubt. And they needed to be fixed in the Gemara and in the received text, and they would say them in the academy, and fix them in the Gemara, and our Rabbis would study them orally" (22).
And he brought proof for this from that which we say in *Eruvin* 32b: "You have also fixed it in the Gemara." And again, "Rabbi and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah," meaning, thus His blessed wisdom decreed. And it is clear, in my humble opinion, regarding the intention of our master, the Gaon, of blessed memory, in this new responsum that God has brought to our hand—at any rate, it is as clear to me as the sun—that this new responsum was sent to Kairouan to explain the responsum before us.
And this is one of the things that are proof for what I will write later, that certainly this precious responsum of Rav Sherira caused several questions, which they asked of him or of his precious son, that they should explain several matters from it for them. Among them was this matter of the Oral Law, whether it was written and hidden away or not, and he answered them with what we find from the mouth of Rabbeinu Shmuel HaNagid, who drank of his waters.
Iyye Ha-Yam: And see what I wrote above in *siman* 20 regarding emending the word "and Rabbi" which is written in other versions, as explained there; my intention was to leave it because of the calculation of the years. But now I see that, besides the fact that here too Rav Sherira says "Rabbi and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah"—and if so, with three instances it constitutes a *hazakah* (presumption)—and besides the fact that it is an established Talmudic text before us in all the aforementioned places, there is also the fact that I do not find such a long period of time from Hillel to Rabbi, unless we calculate from the day of the birth of Hillel the Elder, in which case the calculation is indeed found to be precise. On the contrary, we need the other versions, in which "and Rabbi" is written, so that we arrive at 301 [years], for Rabbi Natan was in the time of Rabban Gamliel of Yavneh and his son, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as is known, and be precise. And I have not seen anyone who has taken note of this matter, that Rabbi Natan preceded Rabbi. And were I not afraid, I would have emended the text to "Rabbi and Rabbi Yonatan," who was a student of Rabbi, and who is truly the end of the Tannaim, and we have many statements from him like a Tanna. And this responsum will prove this emendation, for the Gaon, of blessed memory, concludes with Rabbi Yonatan to inform us that he did not teach anything from our Mishnah, but only that which was taught before him, and he supports this by saying, "as is explained in his book, etc." If so, from this it is proven that he had the textual version "Rabbi and Rabbi Yonatan" in the Talmudic texts as well. But what can we do, as it is not in our power to emend the Talmud.
Or, we could say that the primary version is that of the *Yuhasin*, page 104, column 1, which reads "Rabbi Natan" in the responsum before us. And so I find in the *Kuzari*, part 3, *siman* 67, that after he recounted the generation of Rabbi Meir, whose colleague was Rabbi Natan the Babylonian, as is known, he wrote as follows: "And after these was Rabbi, who is Rabbeinu HaKadosh, who is Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, and with him were Rabbi Natan and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korhah," etc., see there. And the author of *Otzar Nehmad* already sensed how it is possible to count Rabbi Natan the Babylonian in the generation of Rabbi, and he struggled greatly with it, see there at length.
But according to this version before us in the *Yuhasin*, it works out well, for the Gaon explicitly says: "And from there," which is to say, "Who is Rabbi Natan?" It emerges that this is not the well-known Rabbi Natan, the colleague of Rabbi Meir, for he was from Babylonia. Furthermore, he makes him a colleague of the sons of Rabbi and of Rabbi Yoshiya of Usha, and it is impossible that this would be Rabbi Natan the Babylonian. And we are forced to say that this is a different, later Rabbi Natan, who was from the Land of Israel, and that his name was not Rabbi Yonatan. And it is concerning this later Rabbi Natan that they said, "Rabbi and Rabbi Natan are the end of the Mishnah." And regarding this, the Gaon rightly supports himself with this statement, and it is fitting to place Rabbi before Rabbi Natan, since he is the later one, for if it were not so, the Talmud should have said, "Rabbi Natan and Rabbi are the end of the Mishnah."
And the words of the *Haver* [author of the Kuzari], who preceded Rav Sherira Gaon by about 207 years, are resolved, when he says: "and with him was Rabbi Natan, secondary to him." And it is a mitzvah for the meticulous scholars to deal with this, to sift the grain from the chaff. And as for what the *Haver* completed there, that after them (Rabbi and his colleagues) were the masters of the Mishnah who are called Tannaim—which the *Otzar Nehmad* wanted to attribute to a scribal error—in my humble opinion, these are matters clarified by the words of the Gaon here. That in truth, the generation after Rabbi are called masters of the Mishnah and Tannaim, even though they were after Rabbi, and they did not have the authority to add to the Mishnayot, and they did not teach anything from our Mishnah, but only that which was taught before Rabbi. And this is the novel insight of the *Haver*, and consider it well, for it is the truth. And with this, my own contribution ends.
22) In the version of the *Yuhasin* it is written "and we study them in the Gemara, the Rabbis," and in *Hofesh Matmonim* "and we study them, all the Rabbis." I, the author of *Iyye Ha-Yam*, have chosen, in place of "in the Gemara" which is in the *Yuhasin*, and in place of "all of them" which is in *Hofesh Matmonim*, the reading "orally" (*be'al peh*). And the reason for this is that we have seen the pattern of the Gaon's practice in this epistle, that immediately after he informs us of the arrangement, composition, and permanent fixing of the text, he uses the term "orally." What is before us? How many times does he say "to fix them"? And immediately, as if in a hurry, he will say "and to study them," "and they study," and "and the Rabbis are careful to study them orally," which appears later. Is this not in order to inform us of the practice of writing, and the prohibition of studying from a written text? And therefore, here too he immediately wrote "and the Rabbis study them orally." And in the Sephardic script customary among us to this day, there is room for error between one who writes the abbreviation *b.'p.* (for *be'al peh*) and one who writes *b.g.m.* (for *be-gemara*), for *be'al peh* would be written as *b.'p.*, and *be-gemara* would be written as *b.g.m.* And the first copyist of the *Yuhasin* erred, and in place of *b.'p.* he wrote *be-gemara* in full, without abbreviation. And the copyist of *Hofesh Matmonim*, since he saw that the word *be-gemara* did not make much sense, especially since it is understood that this is study from the fixed order, substituted "all of them" (*kulehu*) in place of *be-gemara*. And in my opinion, this is a true conjecture upon which it is proper to rely, especially for one who is expert in the Sephardic script customary among us. And the copy of the *Yuhasin* is Sephardic from birth and from the womb, and its appearance testifies to it; it brings forth its stream, turning flint to a fountain, through the wisdom of its precise script, language, and arrangement of matters.
