התמודדות עם ראיות השד"ל: ביאור סוגיות התלמוד — Addressing Shadal's Proofs: Elucidation of Talmudic Passages
שערי תשובה סי' קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

נפתלי על המקרא, ואם תורה שבכתב היא והוה מצינן להו למימר תא ספרא ונחזי, אלא הכל יודעים כמה נקל לנפול הטעות בספרים הכתובים וכו' ובזה אין להאריך כי למפורסמות אין צריך ראיה" עכ"ד. ושפתים ישק משיב דברים נכוחים, ועל שאר דבריו אין להאשימו מטעם האמור ודוק זאת שנית מי לא עסקינן שכמה דברים נשארו ע"פ ולא נכתבו, אבל שהיו מעט, ונפל בהו הספק הזה היכי תנן? וחדא מהן ההיא דעירובין דנ"ג לפי הנוסח שהביא ר"ש גאון באגרתו החמודה הנז"ל דר' עצמו הוא דשאיל "איכא איניש דלשאיל להו לבני יהודה מאברין תנן או מעברין תנן וכו' אי מלשון עיבור או מלשון אבר, וסיים הגאון דאלמא לר' גופיה דתרציה למתניתין מספקא ליה, ובתלמודין שלפנינו כתוב "א"ר אבא איכא מאן דמשאיל וכו', ואין ספק שנפל הטעות מן איכא" "לאבא" ושוב כדי לכוין הלשון הוסיפו גם מלת "איכא" ונעשה "א"ר אבא איכא" וכו' והאמת הוא "אמר רבי איכא מאן דלשאיל" ובזה סרה קושית הגאון הרמ"ז בחק"ל יו"ד דש"י ע"ד ודוק, ותמהני על בעלי הגהות איך עד היום לא נרגשו מטעות זה, אלא דמצד אחד אני תמיה על החידוש הזה, שהתלמוד יביא מימרא אחת בשם "אמר רבי" כאשר יביא מימרת אמורא אחד מבלי שיאמר "תנן" או תניא" או "תנא" שודאי התלמוד מאיזה ברייתא שאב דבר זה והוא פלא (לז) לכל אפייא, אל גירסת

English Translation

"He gives a kiss to the lips" who offers upright words. And concerning his other statements, he cannot be blamed for the aforementioned reason. And examine this again: Are we not dealing with many matters that remained oral and were not written down, but that they were few, and this doubt fell upon them—how do we learn them? And one of these is that passage in Eruvin, folio 53, according to the version cited by Rabbi Shemu'el Gaon in his aforementioned cherished epistle, that Rabbi himself inquired, "Is there anyone who can ask the inhabitants of Yehudah: Do we learn 'me'abrin' (from 'intercalation') or 'me'avrin' (from 'limb')?" etc., whether it is from the root of 'intercalation' or from the root of 'limb'. And the Gaon concluded that it appears that Rabbi himself, who provided the explanation for the Mishnah, was in doubt concerning it. However, in our Talmuds, it is written: "Rabbi Abba said: Is there anyone who asks?" etc. And there is no doubt that the error crept in from 'ikha' to 'Abba', and again, in order to align the language, they also added the word 'ikha', and it became "Rabbi Abba said, is there anyone," etc. The truth is: "Rabbi said: Is there anyone who asks?" And in this manner, the difficulty raised by the Gaon Rabbi Mosheh Zakut in his work *Hakol Yod'u Dashim al Rabbi* is resolved; examine this. And I am astonished at the masters of glosses how, until today, they have not sensed this error. However, on the one hand, I am perplexed by this novelty, that the Talmud would cite a single saying in the name of "Rabbi said," as it would cite the saying of an Amora, without stating "Tanan" (we learned in a Mishnah), or "Tanya" (it was taught in a Baraita), or "Tana" (a Tanna taught). For certainly the Talmud drew this matter from some Baraita, and this is a source of wonder to all, according to the Gaon's textual variant.
And again, I saw that the Tosafot in Avodah Zarah, folio 34a, under the dictum "ve-im ikal'a le-Ginzek," wrote that Rashi in Ta'anit, folio 11, substituted Mar Ukva for Rabbi Akiva, for it is inconceivable that Rabbi Akiva would be in doubt concerning such simple matters. Furthermore, the Talmud should have presented it in the language of a Baraita. Nevertheless, this is not difficult, for we find in many places that it mentions an incident concerning Rabbi Akiva and the like. Thus far their words.
Behold, we find that the Tosafot are of the opinion that the Talmud is accustomed to bringing matters from the Tannaim and their sayings without the language of "Tanan" or "Tanya" or the like, but rather brings them as it brings a matter or saying from an ordinary Amora. This is true, for the language of Rashi in Ta'anit already proves this, as Rashi's main inquiry is only concerning the order of the language, that "Rabbi Akiva happened upon," etc., which is the language of the Talmud and not the language of a Baraita. For the language of a Baraita is "An incident concerning Rabbi Akiva," etc. And I cannot, in my hurried pace, search if anyone has aroused himself to resolve this language of Rashi. Be that as it may, at any rate, we find that the Tosafot testify in their greatness that we find many such instances in the Talmud. And indeed, it is true that many events and sayings of the Tannaim are brought anonymously in the Talmud without any language from which it is understood that it is a Baraita or Mishnah or Tosefta. And see Shabbat, folio 21a, "Rav Huna said in the name of Rabbi," in the manner it brings the sayings of the Amoraim. And similarly, there, on folio 32b, Rabbi Me'ir and Rabbi Yehudah disagree concerning it, one said, etc., and the other said, etc., and this is precisely the language of the later Talmud. And similarly, there, on folio 46, and Betzah, folio 40, "Rabbi Shim'on bar Rabbi asked Rabbi anonymously." And thus all those matters recounted at the beginning of the chapter "Ha-Po'alim" concerning Rabbi Eli'ezer bar Rabbi Shim'on and Rabbi. And similarly, concerning the incident of "Oven of Akhnai" (Bava Metzi'a, folio 59b), "the wife of Rabbi Eli'ezer, the sister of Rabban Gamli'el," etc. All of these are, in their entirety, events that occurred to great Tannaim, and not only does the Talmud bring them without prefacing "Tanan" or "Tanya" or "Tana," but furthermore, it arranges the entire narrative in purely Talmudic language: "Rabbi happened to the place of Rabbi Abba bar Shim'on," "to the place of Rabbi Tarfon" (ibid., folio 85), and not in pure sacred tongue as is the language of the Tannaim, as is known.
And all this is a decisive proof for the words of the aforementioned Tosafot. For if it were not for the simple doubts which it is impossible to imagine that Rabbi Akiva did not know how to resolve, there would be no justification to change the textual variant merely due to a difference in language. And even though the Talmud recounts it in the language "Rabbi Akiva happened upon," which is common to the later Talmud, and not in the language "An incident concerning Rabbi Akiva" which is the language of a Baraita, since we find this in many places. However, since it has come to hand here: Rabbi taught, "One does not pass over precepts." It is proper to pause here for a moment to understand the reason for these matters: How are these matters different, which are brought in the aforementioned manner, from many matters and laws brought in the language of a Baraita and Mishnah, and with the mention of the compilation's name, such as "Tanan" or "Tana" or "Tanya" and the like? However, according to the true principle we established, that all Mishnayot, Baraitot, and Toseftot were already arranged, written, and preserved in readiness; therefore, when the compiler of the Talmud is compelled to bring one of them to establish and embed it in the Talmud, he copies it in that same language and in that same style, and also mentions the name of the compilation from which he copied it, using one of the expressions appropriate for that compilation, whether "Tanan" or "Tanya" or "Tana" or "Ta Shema" (come and hear), or "Raminhu" (they contradict him), and the like. As we shall further elaborate on the matter of a hidden scroll. And since the Talmud is merely a copier, therefore, it does not use the language of the Tanna himself, which was a pure sacred tongue, but rather neither adds nor subtracts out of reverence and the like. For example, the incident where Ben Zakkai examined fig stalks, which Rabbi found as such and copied as such. And similarly, what was necessitated above in section 39, when the Talmud was compelled to bring the Baraita with Ze'iri's commentary, which was already written and preserved, it brought it in the language "Ze'iri said," for at that time they referred to him thus, without the title "Rav." But Rava, when he wished to mention him, honored him with all due respect and said, "Every Baraita which Rav Ze'iri did not explain to me," etc. See above and quench your thirst. All this holds true for matters that were already written and preserved in their original form and character. However, many matters that remained oral and were not written down or arranged with a single mouth and a single language until the time of the Amoraim, such as this, indeed, when they are brought to be established in the Talmud, they cannot mention "Tanan" or "Tanya," etc., because they are not found in those compilations. Nor, in the manner of a hidden scroll, as we shall discuss later, was it possible to mention them, for these matters were not found written, but rather were received by a student directly from the mouth of a Rabbi, orally. And since this is the case, the later Amora certainly relates the tradition only in the language he speaks, and likewise the redactor of the Talmud establishes it in the Talmud only in his accustomed language. And this is clear as the sun. In my opinion, we do not need the forced interpretation of the early Tosafot in Yoma, folio 57, who, regarding what is written in the Talmud there concerning the dispute between Rabbi Yoshiya and Rabbi Yonatan in the language "Itamar" (it was stated), wrote that the Tosafot stated that because they were later Tannaim, this language, which is common for Amoraim, was used. And truly, I have well said that it is an immense forced interpretation, for in several places the disputes of these later Tannaim are presented...
