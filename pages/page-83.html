English Translation

Indeed, our French Rabbis would surely retract their opinions without any trace of doubt. And what distinguishes this matter from all other laws and directives upon which the House of Israel relies, whether concerning prohibitions or monetary laws? For we do not declare the Halakhah to be according to the later authorities if the latter had not seen the words of the earlier authorities who preceded him. And this is what Morenu Ha-Rav Ya'akov Qallai wrote in his Responsum, Root 94: "And according to what is learned, it is only in a case where the words of the earlier authorities are written in a known and publicized book that we say the Halakhah is according to the later authorities. But if something is found written in a responsum of a certain Gaon, and its memory has not ascended to a well-known book, even if a later decisor is found who rules contrary to the preceding Gaon, it is possible to say that perhaps that later decisor did not hear his words, and if he had heard them, he would have retracted. And proof can be brought from that which we recite in Tractate Ketubot, Chapter 'Metzi'at Ha-Ishah': 'He hung it for him,' etc. And Rabbeinu Ha-Gaon wrote in a responsum, and these are his words: 'Now we have come to investigate the words of Rabbi Yochanan, who is a later authority. Shall we say that he heard the words of Rabbi, who stated that one may remove assets for sustenance, and he saw a difficulty in them and rejected them? And since Rabbi Yochanan, who is a later authority, rejected them, we do not act according to Rabbi. Or shall we say that he did not hear them, for if he had heard them, he would not have disagreed with him? Come and hear,' etc., until, 'It is thus found that it was not resolved whether he heard it or not. Therefore, due to doubt, we do not attribute a dispute between Rabbi and Rabbi Yochanan.' Thus ends his statement. Here it is clearly stated by Rabbeinu Ha-Gaon that we ought not to attribute a dispute. And if so, we are compelled to say that he did not hear it, etc., 'and if he had heard it, he would not have disagreed.' And this is the reason: since we do not find in any Baraita or teaching in the name of Rabbi that he would say that one may remove assets for sustenance, etc., but rather he responded in a responsum to a query to Rabbi, etc., therefore, there is reason to doubt whether Rabbi Yochanan heard it or not. But if it had been taught in a Baraita in the name of Rabbi, etc., we would surely say that Rabbi Yochanan heard it and did not accept it, and we would rule according to him, etc. And for this reason, the Ge'onim wrote that the Halakhah is according to the later authorities, and not that they had not heard, etc. But concerning what is found in a responsum to a query, like that where Rabbi hung it for him among the wheat, etc., in that case, there is indeed reason to doubt whether he heard it." Thus ends his statement. And concerning this responsum of Morenu Ha-Rav Ya'akov Qallai, it is recorded that all those who came after him relied upon it (among them, refer to Morenu Ha-Rav Moshe Alshikh, Sign 39; Morenu Ha-Rav Shmuel di Medina, Choshen Mishpat, Sign 1; Morenu Ha-Rav Yom Tov Tzahalon, Choshen Mishpat, Sign 53; Simchat Yom Tov, Sign 17; She'eilot u-Devarim, Sign 242, Item 68). The sum total of all that has been stated is that when the matter is a responsum to a query, we say that due to doubt, we do not attribute a dispute, and we assert that they surely did not hear it, and if they had heard it, they would have retracted. And if so, these two responsa from Rabbi Sherira, the aforesaid—one in Yuchasin and one before us in Sha'arei Teshuvah—which were sent to Spain or to Africa, from where shall we find grounds to say that Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki and those who followed him in this opinion heard that not even a single letter of the Oral Torah was committed to writing until the time of the Ge'onim, and the renowned Ge'onim, upon whom the eyes of all Israel were fixed, testify in their greatness to the contrary, and they are not contrary to the roots of the Gemara, God forbid! And as we have extensively explained above, so that the Gaon Rabbi Moshe Zakut, of blessed memory, should not come to say before us "that many words of the Ge'onim were rejected because they were not based on the roots of the Gemara"—for in truth, this is not the case. And if so, nothing remains to oppose this fundamental truth of the Ge'onim's opinion except the opinion of our French Rabbis. And if so, they are later authorities who surely did not hear, for there is a period of less than seventy years between the passing of Rabbeinu Hai Gaon and the passing of Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki. Thus, compelling evidence suggests that they surely did not hear it, and if they had heard it, they would have retracted. Further elaboration on this matter is but weariness of the flesh.
And now, the eminent Gaon, may his light shine, enlightened my eyes and showed me what Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki wrote in Ketubot Folio 7, Side B, and Gittin Folio 40, Side B, for from there it is clearly proven that this cherished epistle was in the possession of Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, of blessed memory. And from this, a candid reproof, better than self-love, emerges for every later scholar not to decide his rulings with complete arrogance, for it is impossible for mortals to be without omissions. Here it is before us: I have passed over the two above-mentioned Talmudic discussions several thousands of times, and I also now remember the responsum of Teshuvot Tashbetz, Part One, Sign 142, Folio 70, Side D, which cites the aforementioned words of Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki. Yet, these aforementioned words of Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki, though read and understood plainly, escaped my notice. Nevertheless, I maintain the upper hand to say that perhaps he did not see it in its entirety as it was, for it is possible that after it reached Qairawan, sections of it were copied to answer specific details among the numerous details written therein. And the proof for this is that responsum below, Sign 71, which is a single spark emanating from the mighty hammer of this cherished epistle, and it is recorded by the copyist that it originates from that great epistle. If so, Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki also had those interpretations as sparks scattered from that epistle, and he knew they were taken from there, and he wrote them with their signs and boundaries. But he never saw the main body of the responsum. And I have proof, for the Tosafot at the beginning of Ketubot, Folio 1, Side C, Dibbur Ha-Matchil 'Peshi'ti', bring from Rabbi Shmuel ben Meir that the author of the She'iltot was at the end of the Amoraim period, and he did not hear that Rabbi Sherira in this epistle of his wrote that he was after several Ge'onim. And refer to Va'ad La-Chakhamim at the beginning of his book and examine it. And I have further a semblance of proof that Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki did not see the entire epistle of Rabbi Sherira in its full form and stature, for in the first chapter of Niddah, Folio 7, Side B, Dibbur Ha-Matchil 'Ha Qama La', he extensively explains that this Gemara refers to the Gemara of the Tannaim, as explained therein. Yet, if he had seen the epistle of our Gaon, which we possess in its entirety, he would have copied his words, as he had extensively explained above in this regard. And also the She'iltot, concerning which the Gaon provided an elucidation, for not even the earliest Rishonim...
