הענף הב': ביאור אגרת רב שרירא גאון — Branch Two: Elucidation of Rav Sherira Gaon's Epistle
שערי תשובה סי' קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

הוא חכמת הראשונים דסברין ביה טעמי משנה בתר וכו', ולא רבו שלמדו משנה אלא שלמדו תלמוד וכו' עד זוהמא לסטרון' וכו' ואחר שבירר הגאון ז"ל איך הוה להו לראשונים תלמוד, ואיך ומה היה התלמוד שלהם, ואיך גם כן לא היו קורין אופן לימוד זה כי אם בשם תלמוד, כמו שקורין אותו האחרונים ממש, ע"ז הוכרח לברר דהגם שהיה להם תלמוד והמשא והמתן והדומה, עכ"ז יברר שלא היה חיבור מסודר וכתוב לפניהם בשום אופן לא על עיקרי הדינים שהם המשנה והברייתא, ולא על המשא והמתן והטעמים שכללם הוא התלמוד, וע"ז התחיל וכתב "ולא הוה להון להנך קמאי, עד דאפטר רבי חיבור כתוב וגרסינ ליה על פה אלא כגון פירושי כתיבי להו (טו) דמפרשינן השתא אנחנא

English Translation

It is the wisdom of the early authorities, who maintain therein the reasoning of the Mishnah after..., and not many studied Mishnah, but rather studied Talmud..., until 'filth to the side', etc. And after the Gaon, of blessed memory, clarified how the early authorities possessed Talmud, and what their Talmud was, and how they also did not call this mode of study by any other name but 'Talmud,' precisely as the later authorities call it, it was therefore necessary for him to clarify that even though they had Talmud, with its dialectics and similar discussions, nevertheless, he would clarify that there was no organized and written composition before them in any manner whatsoever, neither concerning the fundamental principles of law, which are the Mishnah and the Baraita, nor concerning the dialectics and the reasoning, the sum of which constitutes the Talmud. And concerning this, he began and wrote: "And for those early ones, until Rabbi departed, there was no written composition, and we studied it orally, but rather like interpretations written for them, which we now interpret for our students of language, 'He returned and expounded...'" Meaning, the author of the first discourse retracted his statement, and this is a well-known matter throughout the Talmud. And indeed, the author of *Ha-Chofesh* (the Inquirer) stretched forth his hands from those who artfully obscured [the text] through pleasant rendition, and wrote: "And when Rabbi saw this, he expounded to them," etc. And he concealed the word "returned" (הדר), which ought not to be done among true critics. Furthermore, the words of the Gaon himself, and the entire expanse of his words, proclaim aloud this notorious error. For his primary proof from this Baraita is to compel the conclusion that after the redaction of the Mishnah, they were compelled to engage in Talmud in order to explain the precise nuances of the Mishnah and to explicate the work of our holy Rabbi. For he indeed states: "And after Rabbi inscribed the Halakhah and his Mishnah, it was the Talmud through which they interpreted the precise nuances of the Mishnah," etc., etc. "As our Rabbis taught," etc., etc. And we say concerning it: "This very point is difficult. You said concerning the Talmud, 'There is no better measure than it,' " etc. It is thus found that his primary proof is that after the redaction of the Mishnah, everyone abandoned the Mishnah and followed the Gemara. And if this Mishnah was taught in the days of Rabbi Akiva, on the contrary, from there is proof to the reverse, that after the redaction of the Mishnah, this eagerness ceased; meaning, it is not necessary, or there is something more necessary, and that is the Mishnah. And this is a stronger proof that this Mishnah was taught in the days of Rabbi, and upon it the Gaon relies to prove that after the sealing of the Mishnah, the eagerness for studying Talmud increased in order to savor the taste of the precise details of the Mishnah, its arrangement, and its concise form, with immense wisdom. And what he brought previously as proof that before Rabbi there was Talmud, he did not rely solely on this, for he already knew that it was taught in the days of Rabbi, as he wrote nearby and clearly, and upon this, he firmly planted a peg in a reliable place. Rather, he began to bring forth that from the generation of the destruction of the Temple, there was already Talmud, generation after generation. And he brought first the teaching of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai, and then the teaching that Rabbi Yehoshua was diligent in Talmud in the generations after him, and the generation after him is the generation of Rabbi. This Mishnah was taught: "Everyone followed the Gemara," for from this, it is also explicitly proven that the study of the Gemara was known from ancient times, and in the days of Rabbi, many inclined after it in order to savor through its power the flavors and precise details of the Mishnah. For if the study of the Talmud was new in that generation, he should not have said, "And everyone followed the Talmud," but rather should have said, "Everyone began to establish themselves in the Gemara," or something similar. And from the fact that he did not say so, it is proven that the study of the Talmud was known to them from the days of the early Tannaim. And this is the substance of the Gaon's proof above, in the manner of every speaker and author who relies upon their arguments in every possible way, according to the parable of Rabbi Akiva in Bava Kamma: "If he finds great ones, he takes; if he finds small ones, he takes." So too, the Gaon, after he proved from those teachings of Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai and Rabbi Yehoshua that there was Talmud in the early days, also used this Mishnah that was taught in the days of Rabbi, for from it, it is explicitly proven that the Talmud was not a new thing in his days. And immediately upon this, he proceeded to bring that Mishnah as a powerful proof for what he innovated for us: that after the conclusion of the Mishnah, the eagerness for Talmud increased to a remarkable extent, until it was said in his days, "There is no better measure than it," because the Mishnah was understood and arranged before them through the sharpness of the Talmud. And because of this, everyone followed it, and the Mishnayot were almost in danger of being forgotten, since all their recitation was oral and it was forbidden to write them down. And concerning this, "He returned and expounded to them: 'Be swift in Mishnah.' " And why should I further elaborate on this matter? For the deceptions of the author of *Ha-Chofesh* in all that he wrote there concerning this, and the stubbornness of his manifest proofs, and all this in order to praise his merchandise, in which every blemish of error and deficiency is found. These compelled me to elaborate so extensively. And enough of this.
And again, when the introduction of Rabbeinu ha-Meiri to *Avot* came to my hand, some of his words were brought below, and I thoroughly clarified that the progression of ha-Meiri’s introduction is none other than this epistle of Rabbi Sherira Gaon, which, with precision, also finds support from this Mishnah, "He returned and expounded to them," etc., in the manner that it appears in the version before us in *Yuchasin* with immense precision, and thus he states on page 24a: "And so too the Talmud itself: even from the earliest generations, they had Talmud, with dialectics concerning the Halakhot which they recited and studied orally, concerning the thirteen hermeneutic principles by which the Torah is expounded, and concerning everything necessary for this. And as it is written in *Sukkah* page 28a regarding Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai, who studied 'the *Havayyot* of Abaye and Rava,' meaning everything necessary for Talmud, even what they were destined to debate concerning those who came after them, and to settle the matters, and this is what they called 'a small matter.' And as it is written in the Talmud in *Bava Metzia* page 33a, 'There is no greater measure than this.' And they raised an objection against it from what was said, 'Be swift in Mishnah more than in Talmud,' and they resolved it: this Mishnah was taught in the days of Rabbi, for he saw that they were neglecting the Mishnah, and he taught them thus so that their Mishnah would be arranged in their mouths." Until here are his words. Observe, dear reader! If the ideas and words of Rabbi Sherira Gaon, of blessed memory, in his epistle found in the version of *Yuchasin* within Rabbeinu ha-Meiri's commentary are not before you, then indeed his entire introduction follows this same course. And we learn explicitly that this Mishnah was taught in the days of Rabbi, and we also learn that even after it was written down, study from written text was forbidden. And therefore, this Mishnah, "Be swift in Mishnah," was taught so that their Mishnah would be arranged in their mouths. And the matters are clarified as a garment. Praise be to the blessed God. And now, being in Vienna, I examined the manuscript there, which is based on the version of *Chiddushei Maimonides*, and here it is written explicitly: "It was taught in the days of Rabbi," etc. And nothing is left for the author of *Ha-Chofesh* but his toil. In the version of *Chiddushei Maimonides*, it is written: "And for those early ones, until Rabbi departed, we studied it orally by common consent, but rather like interpretations which we, and those similar to us, now interpret for our students, all of whom have learned it. And this is written in one aspect, and this is written in another aspect, as is proper." Here, I adjure the pleasant reader to don the mantle of righteous critique as armor to ascertain this matter, to see with discerning eyes who is the one who adds and who is the one who subtracts—meaning, whether the transcriber of the epistle brought in *Yuchasin* is the one who adds any expression of writing in the marginalia of the author of *Ha-Chofesh*, page 3, or if the transcriber of his epistle in the name of *Chofesh Mechuphas* is the one who subtracts. And there is no doubt that the transcriber in *Ha-Chofesh* is certainly found guilty.
