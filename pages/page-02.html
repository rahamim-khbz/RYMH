אי"י הי"ם
על דא ודאי ברוכי מברכינן ברוך פוקח עורים! שהיא התשובה כדמותה כצלמה, אשר הובאה בריש תשו' שערי צדק כמערכה אל הדרוש, אשר איזה מחכמי הדור הע"י קראו עליה ערער, ומבלי השקפה הנאותה גרשוה מהסתפח בנחלת ה' וכבר הובא סוף תשו' זאת מדיבור "וחכמתם ופלפוליהם" עד סוף התשו', בשו"ת מוהר"מ אלשקאר סי' ג"ן וסוף סי' ס"ח דמות שורו"ת ממש, ובתלתא הויא חזקה, ולקמן בעז"ה בסוף דברינו בענף הה' יבא הדיבור כזה בבירור עצום.
אבל טרם כל דבר חובה עלינו לעמוד ולבאר דברים אלו שיצאו מקולמוס גאוננו ז"ל: "ושית סדרי משנה דאתגניזו מיומי דהלל ושמאי" מאי האי? תורה שבע"פ נותנה ליכתב בימים הראשונים? והלא גם את אחרונים איסורא רבינו עליהו עד תחילת זמן הגאונים וכמו שיבא? וכדי לבא לידי בירור וזיקוק הענין האדיר הזה גמרתי כדעתי לחלקו לה' ענפים כדי להקל מעל הקורא בכל מה שאוכל, הענף הא' יתטפל להביא סברות החולקים אם תושבע"פ נכתבה או לא, ולהכריע בראיות מן התלמוד בבלי וירושלמי, ומדרשים שלכל הפחות אין שום חד מהצדדים יש לו ראיה מופתית מהתלמודים והמדרשים דכוותיה אזלא ריהטא דשמעתתא וכיון שכן הדין נותן להשמע אל הגאונים אשר הם ירשו את מורשיהם חכמי התלמוד, ואחר דאתברר דמן התלמוד ליכא שום ראיה מופתית לחד מהצדדים, א"כ לגאונים משפט הבכורה והם הנכנסים ראשונה להודיע לנו מה, ומי, ואיך, ובפרט בדבר הנוגע לקורות תושבע"פ. והענין עצמו יביאנו להתטפל בענף הב', והוא בביאור אגרת רב שרירא גאון הובאה ביוחסין הידועה והמפורסמת אם נוכל לברר מתוכה בראיות מופתיות בחד מהב' צדדים, הענף הג' יטפל בדעת שאר גדולי עולם כר"ש הנגיד, והרמב"ם, והראב"ד והמאירי אם דבריהם ברור מללו, ואם ראוי לסמוך עליהם, הענף הד' יטפל בספרים שבאו לי מן החדש אחר שגמרתי ענין זה על מתכונתו אם להימין אם להשמאיל ע"כ מה שהעלינו אנן בעניותין,

English Translation

A disciple of the disciples of even the least among the Geonim—for we have found regarding one of the Geonim that after he had departed this life, it was found concerning him (may that man be remembered for good) that he possessed all the dialectical inquiries of the former Rabbis with one another, as well as the six orders of the Mishnah which had been hidden since the days of Hillel and Shammai, and numerous matters without measure, such that even in the Heavenly Academy they establish the Halakhah according to his view. How then do foxes who have never beheld the luminaries presume to speak perversely against the Lord and His inheritance? He who sifted the entire Talmud through thirteen sieves and mastered all that has existed from the days of Yehoshua bin Nun, transmitted from man to man as Halakhah le-Moshe mi-Sinai—shall one then attribute error to even the least among the disciples of the Geonim? (It appears that the words are not perfectly joined, yet they are understood). For all of them are the words of the living God, and even in the house of study of Moshe, our teacher and the master of the prophets, they would not be rejected; indeed, their wisdom and their dialectics are the very matter which the Lord commanded to them from their places through Moshe. And though they may assert that “such is the case” without adducing proof of its source, one must entertain no doubt regarding matters small or great; for whosoever disputes any of their words is as one who disputes against the Lord and His Torah.
Iyye Ha-Yam: Assuredly, we must utter the benediction, "Blessed be He who openeth the eyes of the blind!" For this is the responsum in its very image and likeness, which was adduced at the beginning of the responsa *Sha'arei Tzedek* as an introduction to the inquiry. Some of the contemporary scholars (may the Lord protect and preserve them) have raised objections against it, and without proper consideration, have cast it out from "cleaving to the inheritance of the Lord." Now, the conclusion of this responsum, from the words "and their wisdom and their dialectic" (*ve-chokhmatam u-pilpuleihem*) to the end of the responsum, has already been cited in the *Responsa of Rabbenu Moshe Alashkar*, section 53, and at the end of section 68, practically line for line; and a threefold occurrence establishes a presumption (*be-taltah hovei chazakah*). Later, with the help of Heaven, at the conclusion of our treatise in the fifth branch, this matter will be discussed with utmost clarity.
First and foremost, it is incumbent upon us to analyze and clarify the following words which emerged from the pen of our Gaon, of blessed memory: "and six, indeed, orders of the Mishnah which were hidden from the days of Hillel and Shammai." What is the meaning of this? Was the Oral Torah permitted to be committed to writing in early times? Surely even upon the later authorities this prohibition was imposed, until the beginning of the Geonic period, as will be shown.
To arrive at a clear and refined understanding of this momentous subject, I have resolved to divide my discussion into five branches, in order to facilitate the reader's task as far as possible.
The first branch will concern itself with adducing the opinions of the contending authorities as to whether the Oral Torah was committed to writing or not, and demonstrating from the evidence of the Babylonian Talmud, the Jerusalem Talmud, and the Midrashim, that at the very least neither of the opposing sides possesses an apodictic proof from the Talmuds or the Midrashim that can be said to represent the uncontested flow of the talmudic discussion (*rihata de-shema'tata*). Consequently, reason dictates that we must defer to the Geonim, who directly inherited the tradition of the sages of the Talmud. Since it is demonstrated that no definitive proof can be drawn from the Talmud in favor of either side, the right of primacy (*mishpat ha-bekhorah*) belongs to the Geonim; they are the primary authorities to instruct us as to the what, the who, and the how, particularly in matters concerning the history of the Oral Torah.
This very subject will lead us in the second branch to an exposition of the famous Epistle of Rav Sherira Gaon, preserved in the well-known *Sefer ha-Yuhasin*, to ascertain whether we can extract from it apodictic proofs in support of either of the two views.
The third branch will treat of the opinions of other leading authorities, such as Rabbenu Shmuel ha-Nagid, Maimonides, Abraham ben David, and Ha-Meiri, investigating whether their assertions are unequivocally stated and if they may be relied upon.
The fourth branch will examine works that have recently come into my possession after I had already structured this inquiry, to see whether they incline to the right or to the left of the conclusions we, in our intellectual poverty, have reached.
Once these four branches have prepared the groundwork for our inquiry, the fifth branch will arrive at the exposition of the present responsum, which is our primary objective. Here we shall demonstrate, on the basis of our extensive discussion in the preceding four branches as well as other apodictic proofs which we shall muster, that this responsum did indeed issue from the pen of our Gaon, and that there is not even a dreamlike suspicion, nor any exaggeration, of forgery.
I shall immediately proceed to the first branch, and I say: I shall not conceal that this wondrous inquiry was already addressed by the celebrated Gaon, the Master of the Land of Israel, my revered grandfather, in his awesome work *Chikre Lev*, *Orach Chayyim*, folio 12b, and *Yoreh De'ah*, Part I, folio 270a, who stood firm to express astonishment at Maimonides, of blessed memory, in his introduction to the *Yad* [i.e., *Mishneh Torah*], where he wrote: "that until Rabbeinu Ha-Kadosh, head of the Beth Din, or prophet..."
