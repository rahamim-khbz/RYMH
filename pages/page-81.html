הענף הה': סיכום וחיזוק דעת הגאונים — Branch Five: Summary and Reinforcement of the Geonic View
שערי תשובה סימן קפ"ז

צילום כתב היד / Scan

נוסח עברי

שעלה בידינו הבירור הזה כשמש בחצי השמים, שנוסח ח"מ נכתב בצרפת או באשכנז, כבר הגענו לפרשת דרכים, וכבר האיר היום לפנינו להחליט שבק"ע נשלחה יד באגרת החמודה הזאת, כי ידוע ומפורסם כמה עלולים חכמי צרפת למהר שלל חש בז ולשלוח יד בגירסאות הקדמוניות כאשר צעקה העז"רה צעקות מבהילות הלא הם כתובות על ספר הישר לר"ת בסופו ובפרט בהקדמתו היקרה, וא"כ השערתנו יצאת לאור שבק"ע השחית והוסיף וגרע כדי לכוין תשו' זאת עם דעת רבותינו הצרפתים דס"ל דלא היו כותבים אפילו אות אחת חוץ ממגלת סתרים עד תחי' זמן הגאונים ואפשר שלא ראום רבותינו הצרפתים תשו' זאת של הגאונים מעיקרא כי אפשר וקרוב לודאי שלספרד או לאפריקא נשלחה, שמשם היו משיאין משואות וטומוס של שאלות, וע"כ אינו מן הנמנע שרבותינו הצרפתים, כיון שלא ראו תשו' זאת נגררו אחר פשטי הסוגייאות "דאי אתה כותב הלכות" הוא על כתיבה ממש, ולא על למוד מתוך הכתב, ואלו היו רואים תשו' הגאונים דבקיאי טפי מנייהו הוו דייקי לפרש פשטי כל הסוגייאות כאשר פירשנום בלי שום מעיק, ושוב דקדקתי היטב בדברי הגאון רב כהן צדק נר"ו בהקדמתו הנז' ד"י ע"ב בדברו על דאבון נפש אשר חל להיות בחשבוננו מהשבושים אשר נפלו באגרת החמודה הזאת, סיים בהחלט וז"ל: "וכפי דעתנו איזה מהם היו כן בעצם, וראשונה מהמחבר עצמו או ממעתיקים הראשונים, היינו כי אפשר כאשר בקשו מהמחבר עצמו העתקה ממכתבם היקר ההוא, שינה ותקן בעצמו, או איזה מעתיק בקירוואן" ע"כ. ועם שאני עדין לא מצאתי מפורש מהיכן בא לו להגאון שי"ר נר"ו להחליט כן שהכתב הראשון נשלח לקירוואן? עכ"ז אליו שומעים, וסהדא רבא פרת, שמימיו פרים ורבים בענינים אלו, גמירי, הלא דבר הוא שכותב בהחלטה גדולה (מ"ג) וא"כ נתקיימה השערתי שכיון שנשלחה לספרד או לאפריקא, ע"ז לא ראוה רבותינו הצרפתים הראשונים,

English Translation

That this clarification has become clear to us like the sun at noon, namely that the version of *Chiddushei ha-Me'iri* was written in France or Ashkenaz, we have already reached a crossroads, and the day has already dawned before us to decide that a hand was evidently laid upon this cherished epistle. For it is known and well-publicized how prone the Sages of France were to hasten to seize and despoil, and to lay a hand upon ancient versions, as the *Ezerah* cried out with alarming cries, are they not written in *Sefer ha-Yashar* by Rabbeinu Tam at its end, and especially in its precious introduction? If so, our hypothesis has come to light that the *Chiddushei ha-Me'iri* evidently corrupted, added, and subtracted in order to align this responsum with the opinion of our French Rabbis who held that they would not write even a single letter, save for a hidden scroll, until the beginning of the Geonic period. It is also possible that our French Rabbis did not see this responsum of the Geonim from the outset, for it is possible, and almost certain, that it was sent to Spain or Africa, from where they would send beacons and missives of questions. Therefore, it is not impossible that our French Rabbis, since they did not see this responsum, confined themselves to the plain meaning of the *sugyot*, understanding "you shall not write *halakhot*" to refer to actual writing, and not to learning from a written text. Had they seen the responsum of the Geonim, who were more expert than they, they would have precisely interpreted the plain meaning of all the *sugyot* as we have interpreted them, without any difficulty.
And again I meticulously examined the words of the Gaon Rav Kohen Tzedek, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, in his aforementioned introduction, folio 10b, where, in speaking of the grievous sorrow which, in our reckoning, befell the corruptions that appeared in this cherished epistle, he concluded decisively and said: "And in our opinion, some of these were indeed inherent, and initially from the author himself or from the first copyists. That is, it is possible that when they requested a copy of that precious letter from the author himself, he altered and corrected it himself, or that some copyist in Kairouan did so." Thus far. Even though I have not yet found it explicitly stated whence the Gaon, may our master live and reign, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, derived his decision that the first text was sent to Kairouan, nonetheless, we defer to him. And the great witness, the Euphrates, whose waters are fruitful and abundant in these matters, is known. Is it not significant that he writes with great decisiveness? If so, my hypothesis is confirmed that since it was sent to Spain or Africa, our early French Rabbis did not see it.
Again, I found the source from which the Gaon, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, drew, and I confirmed and strengthened it. See above, Section 71, what I wrote there. And when I was in Vienna, presently, in the winter of 5672 [1912 CE], I was in the magnificent and awe-inspiring archive of books of the *Kireh*, and there came into my hand a collection of Geonic responsa, torn and in their original, unembellished state. Among them, I saw the responsum of Rabbi Sherira Gaon, of blessed memory, which we are discussing, and many pages were missing from it. Most of it followed the version of the *Chiddushei ha-Me'iri*, as I demonstrated above in the annotations, but it begins with this formulation: "A question that our teacher, the Rabbi Ya'akov bar Nissim bar Yoshiyahu, from before our master, Rav Sherira Gaon, Rosh ha-Yeshivah of the Exile, asked in the name of the holy community of Kairouan, and he commanded and wrote his responsum to me in the year one thousand, nine hundred and ninety-eight according to the Seleucid era reckoning." And there is no doubt that this very responsum is the one that was in the hand of the author of *Ma'or Einayyim*, chapter 24, and from it he decided that it was sent to Kairouan, for it is written here in the name of the holy community of Kairouan, and in *Ma'or Einayyim* it also states that it was sent to the holy community of Kairouan. Furthermore, the author of *Ma'or Einayyim* added that it was written at its end: "Written in the year one thousand, two hundred and ninety-eight according to the Seleucid era reckoning, in the city of Peroz Shapur." And the Gaon, may our master live and reign, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, in *Toledot Rabbi Nissim*, in the *Ezerah*, end of note 32, raised excellent and sound arguments against this; see his words. And in this manuscript before us in the Vienna treasury, may its Creator preserve it forever, this addition is absent. It is possible it was written at the end and that page is missing, and this is written at the beginning of the question, as your eyes observe.
And now I also recalled the well-known introduction of al-Afodi, which is still in manuscript and part of which is in my possession, where he too, wishing to explain what "Talmud" means as our Sages of blessed memory use it, drew support from the epistle of Rav Sherira before him and wrote thus: "And Rav Sherira wrote in a responsum to the community of Kairouan, in the year 4448: 'And the Talmud, concerning which you inquired...'" I will note in passing that the version before al-Afodi was the *Yuchasin* version and not the *Chiddushei ha-Me'iri* version, as I meticulously ascertained. And if there is a discrepancy of one year, it is of no consequence, for the error arises from the calculation, as the Gaon, may our master live and reign, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, elaborated in *Erekh Millin*.
And now, in the year 5618 [1858 CE], because the printing was delayed due to the loss of some types, may the Lord illuminate our path. The book *Ha-Mafte'ach*, composed by our master Rabbenu Nissim of Kairouan, may his memory protect us, amen, reached our hands. And I saw in his introduction words as clear as a garment, strongly and powerfully upholding all that was written throughout these five sections. Therefore, I decided to transcribe them here, for in my opinion, this book has not been clearly seen in many lands. And primarily, the matter upon which I focused compels me not to allow the reader to wander after other books concerning the clarification of this issue, namely, that the Oral Torah was written down gradually, and everything was studied orally until the time of the Rabbanan Savora'ei. And here is his golden language, relevant to our discussion: "I will explain the time in which the Mishnah and also the Talmud were written. And I say: The field of tradition and transmission was preserved among the nation, and when a person would read 'and they shall make for themselves *tzitzit*,' he would say that this *tzitzit* is *tzitzit*; we place it on the corner of the *tallit*; it has eight threads and five knots, and he knew all the interpretations of *tzitzit* and their matters. All this was preserved by him purely through received tradition and transmission. And similarly, all the commandments, when they would read them, they knew into how many categories each commandment was divided, and what its essence and its interpretation were, and so forth." And the nation did not depart from this custom from the days of Moses until after the destruction of the Second Temple, one hundred and fifty years later, in the days of Rabbi Yehudah ha-Nasi, who was called "Rabbi" and "Our Holy Master," as it is stated in the Jerusalem Talmud, tractate *Sanhedrin*, chapter on those who are strangled, *halakhah* 5: "Rabbi Abbahu said..." and so forth. And this was in the days of Antoninus, king of Edom, and Rabbi Yehudah ha-Nasi was held in great esteem and held great authority with him, as I will explain some of what I find, as already indicated in the forthcoming note on the Karambiyya manuscript, chapter two, section 40, where it was here explained by the Gaon, may our master live and reign, may the light of Israel and its Torah shine, that this was 120 years after the destruction, which is 'Chet-Chet' according to the Seleucid era reckoning, and so forth.
