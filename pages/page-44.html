the last of the Men of the Great Assembly, and the first of the Sages, as we have written. This quality was further extended to him, enabling him to record his affairs, as conveyed by his statements in this tractate. Similarly, what is recounted from his words written in the Talmud, these matters have a bearing upon the chain of tradition concerning the affairs of this tractate, as our Sages of blessed memory stated in Tractate Nedarim 9b: Shimon HaTzaddik said, "Never in my life have I eaten of the asham offering of an impure Nazirite," etc. And after Shimon HaTzaddik, etc. And after the generation of Shemayah and Avtalyon, Hillel HaZaken was appointed Nasi, etc. And this Hillel ascended from Babylonia, etc.
The fifth generation of Tannaim after the Destruction of the Temple is Rabbenu HaKadosh, he who begot, by the compassion of the Lord upon His people, Rabban Shimon whom we mentioned, and whose name is Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, and he is called by all Rabbenu HaKadosh, etc. This Rabbenu HaKadosh received the tradition from his father and from Rabbi Elazar ben Shamua, and he witnessed in his time great troubles spreading and the students diminishing. He therefore compiled the Book of the Mishnah in the six known orders, 150 years after the Destruction of the Temple. He included therein all that was received in the Oral Torah, from interpretations of the Torah and the commandments, and what was innovated in each generation, from what became known to them, along with what he found to be complete tractates among all the Sages, as they said, "On the day Rabbi Akiva ben Ezrach was appointed Nasi, on that very day Tractate Eduyyot was reviewed," and as it is written at the end of Horayot, "To inquire from us, master, matters of Uktzin." This included both that which they did not find arranged and which he arranged anew, and that which he added by the power of his intellect and his reasoning, etc. Likewise, some other Sages immediately thereafter compiled other Baraytot such as Mishnat HaMishkan, Derekh Eretz Rabbah and Zuta, and Tractate Soferim, etc.
And similarly, other Baraytot were compiled in those times of which we are not aware. Bar Kappara likewise innovated a Tosefta in all six orders, similar to the Baraytot of Rabbi Hiyya and Rabbi Oshaya. There is no difference between them at all except in language, neither in arrangement nor in subject matter, save for a few minor places. And this is the reason why those Toseftot are not found complete in our possession today. This is also the reason that many times we find some Baraytot in the Shas that are not found in the Barayta of Rabbi Hiyya and Rabbi Oshaya, and we do not know their source, etc. And similarly, we found a Tanna whose source is unknown to us, because many Sages would create Mishnayot and Baraytot of their own, which we call Toseftot, etc. Even before the time of Rabbi Hiyya, as you always see, "It was taught in the school of Rabbi Ammi," "Rav Shervya taught," in Kiddushin "in the school of Levi," "it was taught in the school of Rabbi Yishmael," "Rabbah bar Mari taught, Bedekah Levi in his Barayta," etc. And likewise, it is always stated in the Shas, "Rabbi So-and-so came and brought a Barayta with him." All these and similar ones are Baraytot that were not established or publicized, but rather each one of them wrote them down as he had received from his teacher, or from what he had learned on his own. For this reason, they did not rely upon them, as they said, "Any Barayta that was not taught in the school of Rabbi Hiyya and Rabbi Oshaya is not a reliable Barayta, and we do not raise objections from it in the study hall," because these were from the foundation of Rabbi, and were copied from his words, while the rest were not publicized compilations, but each individual taught as he desired, etc. And Rabbenu HaKadosh knew everything, as it is written in Nedarim 41a: "Rabbi was learned in thirteen facets of Halakhah, and gathered them by his strength and his wisdom." He clarified what was proper to clarify from the words of his predecessors, along with what he added to them according to his expansive knowledge and great intellect, and he arranged everything one by one. Before him, there was no one who could compose like him or who could act according to his pattern, and after him, there will not be such, etc.
And thereafter came the words of Rabbi Hiyya for further explanation, etc. We shall return to our subject: after Rabbenu HaKadosh compiled the Mishnah, and Rabbi Hiyya compiled the Barayta, some Sages followed after them. Rav compiled a work on the Book of Vayikra and called it Sifra, etc., and he revealed in it the hidden meanings of the Mishnayot pertaining to those subjects. And then Rav descended to Babylonia 150 years after the Destruction of the Temple, and found there Rav Shila, etc. Other compilations were composed that were not mentioned, such as Mekhilta, Pesiqta, Midrash Hashem, and the Book of Midrashim, such as Rabbah and Tanhuma, Midrash Tehillim, Midrash Hazit, Midrash Nehamot, Midrash Temurah, the Sefer HaBahir, Shiur Komah, and Pirkei Hekhalot, etc. And then began the era of the Amoraim, etc., the first generation, etc., and the second generation, etc. After them began the era of the Rabbanan Savorai, etc., and the last of them was Rav Sheshna, a median figure. Would that I knew if there is a difference between Mishnat HaMishkan according to Rabbi Shimon and between the Idra d'vei Mashkena: how this description ascends from the heights of mountains to the heads of the Geonim of the Yeshivot.
